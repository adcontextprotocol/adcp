The account operator is now described as a registrable domain (adcp#8913). `operator` in `core/account-ref.json`, `core/canonical-account-ref.json`, `sync_accounts` and `request_account` is the registrable domain (eTLD+1 under the Public Suffix List, ICANN and private sections) of the organization operating on the brand's behalf, such as `pinnacle-media.com`, not a subdomain such as `team.pinnacle-media.com`, because the brand's `authorized_operators` and trust.json grants name organizations that way. When the brand operates directly, it stays the brand's domain. A seller MAY refuse an operator that is neither a registrable domain nor the brand's domain. For this check, a name under the reserved `.example` TLD (RFC 2606) takes the list's default rule, so `pinnacle-agency.example` is registrable and `team.pinnacle-agency.example` is not. `authorized_operator.domain` in `brand.json`, the deprecated `core/operator-identity.json`, and `paying_principal.operator` in the sponsored-intelligence context say the same. The schema patterns are unchanged, since a pattern can't express "registrable". The brand domain is unchanged. The six storyboards that sent a subdomain of `pinnacle-agency.example` as their isolated operator (`media_buy_seller`, `media_buy_seller/measurement_accountability`, `media_buy_seller/vendor_metric_accountability`, `creative_generative/seller`, `sales_broadcast_tv`, `sales_catalog_driven`) now send `pinnacle-<purpose>.example`, and a storyboard lint keeps every account operator a registrable domain or the brand's own domain. Updated: the Account identity section and Parties table of `docs/accounts/overview.mdx`, the `sync_accounts` and `request_account` field tables, the entity and authorized operators tables in the L2 accounts guide, Authorized Operator Resolution in `docs/brand-protocol/brand-json.mdx`, the brand specialist module, and a pointer in the AAO Verified setup.

This adds a seller permission in the 3.3 beta. A buyer that sends a subdomain operator may now be refused.
