Let `criteria.outcome_target.goal` carry a vendor-measured outcome. A third `goal` branch, `kind: "vendor_metric"` with `vendor` (a `brand-key`, matched on `domain` and `brand_id`) and `metric_id`, mirrors the `vendor_metric` optimization goal, so plan, buy and delivery reconcile on one `(vendor, metric_id)` key. The motivating case is a retailer or QSR planning "5,000 store visits measured by a foot-traffic vendor", optionally at a cost per visit. Additive: every document valid today stays valid. A 3.2 validator rejects the new branch as a schema error, so buyers send it only on a negotiated `adcp_version` of 3.3 or later to a seller whose product declares the pair.

**Answer path.** A vendor goal is answered in each forecast point's `vendor_metric_values[]`, not in `metrics`. The entry carries the goal's `vendor` and `metric_id`, `value` as the flight total at the point's `budget`, and `unit` (required for vendor goals) equal to the vendor's declared unit. `metrics` carries no key for the vendor metric (its keys are `forecastable-metric` and `event-type` values) but every point carries `metrics.spend`, so it is never empty. `forecast_range_unit` is `spend`. The proposal binds the goal as every purchase's primary optimization goal (or `budget_allocation`'s, under seller-optimized allocation) and lists `{ scope: "vendor", vendor, metric_id }` in each purchase's `commercial_terms.reporting_commitments`, since optimizing toward a vendor metric without committing to report it is unverifiable. The prose that said forecast points always carry the goal's key in `metrics` now says so for metric and event goals only.

**Eligibility.** No new capability flag. The seller plans only pairs a product lists in `vendor_metric_optimization.supported_metrics[]` and `reporting_capabilities.vendor_metrics`, matched on the vendor's `domain` and `brand_id`; products that do not declare the pair are left out of the plan. Anything else, including every vendor goal sent to a seller that declares no `vendor_metric_optimization`, is rejected with `INVALID_REQUEST` naming `criteria.outcome_target.goal`, and the seller never substitutes another vendor or metric. `volume` is a total of the metric's declared unit across the flight, so the seller either plans against that total or rejects. It MUST NOT reinterpret a mean, score, or rate as a total or plan a cost target on one. A cumulative count such as store visits is plannable; an average attention score is not. A `metric_id` identifies one methodology, so a vendor publishes a distinct id per attribution window or exposed-versus-control design.

**No implied vendor relationship.** A planning-time vendor goal asserts nothing about whether the buyer holds a contract or data license with the vendor. The seller's declared pair is the only planning-time eligibility test (the discovery SHOULD for the vendor's published catalog still applies at acceptance); anything the seller needs from the buyer to provision the vendor's measurement is an obligation that arises at proposal finalization, like every `outcome_target` answer.

**Cost target on a vendor goal.** `outcome_target.cost_per` binds to the vendor goal by `vendor` and `metric_id`, as `BiddingPolicy.cost_per` already defines for vendor-metric goals. The seller additionally requires `cost_per` in the matching entry's `supported_targets` and answers only inside its advertised `features.bidding_policy` profile; otherwise it rejects with `INVALID_REQUEST` naming `criteria.outcome_target.cost_per`. The seller validates the goal before the cost target. Vendor results often land after the flight, so the cost is reconciled after the fact: delivered `spend` divided by the reported `vendor_metric_values[].value`, compared with the answered amount.

**Event-goal cost targets now have a declaration.** `conversion_tracking.supported_targets` already lists the event-goal target kinds a seller computes against; listing `cost_per` there now also declares that the seller binds `outcome_target.cost_per` to an event goal, and buyers do not rely on an event-goal cost target from a seller that omits it. Sellers that already answer event cost targets without listing it keep working; they skip the new conformance scenario until they list it.

**Reading a cost answer.** The docs now state how a buyer classifies a response without a new field: an amount equal to the ask is plannable (with the forecast volume read against any requested `volume`), a higher amount with the same `strength` is a counter-offer, `INVALID_REQUEST` naming `criteria.outcome_target.cost_per` or `outcome: "rejected"` is a decline. `bidding.cost_per` is never a commitment; a seller commits to a cost through a pricing option that prices the result (for example `cpa`).

**Deferred.** `threshold_rate` and `per_ad_spend` on `outcome_target` and a structured `best_achievable_target` answer are not part of this change.

**Conformance.** Two new scenarios are registered under `sales_proposal_mode`; grading for existing sellers does not change. `media_buy_seller/outcome_target_vendor_metric` is gated on `media_buy.vendor_metric_optimization` and covers the volume answer in `vendor_metric_values[]`, the vendor reporting commitment, a cost cap on the vendor goal (for sellers advertising fixed media-buy cost caps), and the `INVALID_REQUEST` rejection of a pair no product declares. `media_buy_seller/outcome_target` moves to 1.1.1 with narrative changes only. `media_buy_seller/outcome_target_event_cost` is gated on `conversion_tracking.supported_targets` containing `cost_per` and fixed media-buy cost caps, and covers a purchase-event cost cap answered with a bound event source and attribution window.
