# What's new in AdCP 3.3

<Warning>
**3.3 is in its beta cycle.** The latest checkpoint is `3.3.0-beta.3`, published 2026-10-31. Beta.0 publishes the protocol artifacts before SDK support, and beta.1 through beta.3 incorporate integration feedback; a beta is SDK-backed only after SDK support for its exact bundle is published. Use the beta in development and staging, and read the [3.3 beta program](/docs/reference/3-3-beta) before testing. This page lists only what has merged; it is refreshed at each beta cut.

**Three features that shipped in the betas are withdrawn after beta.1: definition pins (beta.0 and beta.1), purchase rights, and installment selection (both beta.1).** All three wait for 4.0, because `commercial-terms` stays closed within 3.x ([details](#withdrawn-from-3-3-planned-for-4-0)).
</Warning>

AdCP 3.3 lets scheduled, human-gated, and offline media run through the same buy as programmatic. A booking can say when it will be confirmed. A proposal request can target a vendor-measured outcome, and a governance plan can budget by period. Products, audiences, and delivery reports can carry what a linear-TV, print, or DOOH seller actually knows: spot lifecycle and makegoods, break position, print proof of insertion and circulation, structured DOOH location, property-grain rollups, and audience sizes as honest ranges. Minute-level dayparts, broadcast mezzanine formats, and the NMO and VideoAmp demographic systems fill in the TV edges.

Around that, 3.3 tightens the places where two conformant 3.2 implementations could still disagree: discovery never provisions an account, one agent-resolution rule covers every signing surface, `required_for` is enforced over A2A with its hardening clauses required, and `reporting_webhook.operation_id` correlation fails closed. Catalog intake becomes an optional declaration, verifiers can advertise A2A `required_for` enforcement through `request_signing.operation_sources`, and `ext.adcp` becomes a reserved namespace. New single-adopter surfaces ship as experimental, so 3.4 can reshape them without breaking stable integrations.

No stable field, enum value, or task is removed. Two result fields, one usage, the principal kind field, the account identity-rekey machinery, and the RFC 9421 signing profiles are deprecated, all functional throughout 3.x. They are `seller_id` on event source and audience results (use `platform_id`), and the natural key as the account reference after provisioning. They also include `account.identity_updates`, `operator_identity`, `ACCOUNT_MOVED`, `ACCOUNT_IDENTITY_CONFLICT`, and `account.stable_account_id` (use `account_id`), `principal_kind` and `expected_principal_kind` (a bound principal is always `buyer_agent`), and the RFC 9421 profiles (use the experimental Web Bot Auth profile). 3.3 adds optional fields, enum values, and capability declarations. Schema-valid 3.2 messages stay schema-valid except for the new domain identifier format, and unless they already used a name 3.3 now defines with a different shape; the list is in the [migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#what-can-fail-validation). The normative narrowings are recorded in [Versioning and governance](/docs/reference/versioning#3-3-exceptions-to-3-x-compatibility-beta) and lead the [migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#required-to-claim-3-3-behavior).

## New in beta.3

beta.3 finishes the reporting-subscription and account-request surfaces, both experimental, against what the TypeScript and Python SDK implementations found. Coverage is now computable from the wire alone, and canonical coverage vectors with an independent reference model pin it. The account change outcomes and the owed confirmation period first shipped in beta.2; they are summarized here with the rest.

**Account changes ([`request_account`](/docs/accounts/tasks/request_account), `account.requests`).**
- A change request resolves one of three ways: `updated` on the named account, `matched` to an account the caller already holds, or `created` as a separate account carrying the change. `created` happens only when the buyer opts in with `allow_separate_account: true`; otherwise a seller that would only accept the change on a new account refuses with `ACCOUNT_CHANGE_REQUIRES_NEW_ACCOUNT`.
- A request that resolves `matched` or `created` now MUST carry `account`, with its `billing`, `billing_entity`, and `payment_terms`, because they can differ from the terms requested. **Adopter action:** sellers that omitted `account` on these resolutions now fail validation ([#8620](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8620)).
- An `updated` request that changed money terms carries `effective_at`, when the new terms take effect. A `created` separate account lists in `not_carried_over` the account-level configurations it doesn't have.
- `currency` and `timezone` change through `request_account` only on sellers that list them in `account.requests.changeable_attributes`; otherwise the change is refused with `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE`.

**Losing authority means losing access ([#8607](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8607)).** After the caller loses authority for an account, every reference to it fails like an unknown identifier, receipts and consumer statuses included. A seller that wants a buyer to finish reconciling, for example after an agency change, keeps a reduced grant listing only the reconciliation tasks instead of revoking. A partial grant that lists `get_reporting_status` but not `sync_reporting_receipts` or `sync_reporting_status` keeps the account covered, and the missing tasks fail with `SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT` ([#8619](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8619)).

**Reporting subscriptions (`media_buy.reporting_subscriptions`).**
- **Which periods are owed.** A buy owes the period in which it is confirmed, in full ([#8593](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8593)). A deactivated subscription reads `inactive` in `subscription_coverage`, with `deactivated_at`, while its obligations are retained ([#8613](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8613)). The flight's stop time uses the new `cancellation_effective_at`, the delivery stop, so delivery through a cancellation notice period stays owed, and a seller delivers on a buy only inside its flight, extending `end_time` first for a make-good ([#8617](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8617)).
- **Billing stays contiguous.** When a cutover hands a billing feed to a schedule with no boundary at the handoff, the stretch is owed as one stub obligation (`stub: true`) instead of going unreported. This covers account timezone changes, and it applies to account-level `sync_accounts` billing configurations too ([#8617](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8617)).
- **Computable from the wire.** `authorization.regranted_at`, a media buy's `flight_history` of retroactive flight edits, each package's `reporting_delivery_offering_ids` fixed at confirmation, and `available_from` / `withdrawn_at` on offerings ([#8618](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8618)).
- **Deterministic.** Resolution is per package and fixed per buy and version until the resolved offering is withdrawn; a buy owes the union of its packages' offerings. `max_accounts` admission is sticky and ordered by `authorization.authorized_at`, then `account_id` in byte order. Billing subscriptions use `match: exact` with `dimensions` ([#8619](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8619)).
- **At agency scale.** `destination_overrides[]` routes accounts to other destinations inside one subscription. Adding or removing accounts no longer needs a new `subscription_version`: membership edits apply in place as `excluded_by_buyer` intervals. The readback lists 16 prior versions and 1,000 accounts inline, with totals, and `get_principal` pages the rest through `reporting_subscription_page`. Several pacing or analytics subscriptions may cover one account; billing stays one per account. Managed-delivery row schemas declare `account_id` and key on it ([#8619](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8619)).
- **Period arithmetic.** A local date's first instant is always a boundary, so `PT24H` equals `P1D` and days nest in sub-daily schedules even where midnight doesn't exist. Two cutovers inside one period and reactivation after a change made while inactive never owe a period twice. `billing_cycle` needs a date component, `P7D` weeks under `utc`, `account_timezone`, and `source_timezone` start on Thursday, `status_retention_days` counts exact 24-hour days, and a `utc` offering matches a `period_timezone` override only when it is exactly `UTC` or `Etc/UTC` ([#8622](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8622)).
- **Test assets.** Canonical coverage vectors and an independent reference model live in `static/compliance/source/test-vectors/reporting-subscriptions/` ([#8616](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8616)), and `comply_test_controller` adds `reporting_subscription_probe` to drive grants, account timezones, and the clock against a remote seller ([#8621](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8621)).

**Live creative updates (experimental, `creative.live_updates`, [#8488](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8488)).** A buyer changes a serving creative by syncing it again under the same `creative_id`. The seller declares, per kind of change, whether it applies `uninterrupted`, `staged` (the approved version keeps serving until the update is approved), or `paused_for_review`, and refuses the rest with `CREATIVE_LIVE_UPDATE_UNSUPPORTED`. `list_creatives` shows `staged_update`, and the opt-in `creative.staged_update_changed` event tracks it.

**Every task response carries the envelope ([#8547](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8547)).** Each task response and async status schema now composes `core/protocol-envelope.json`, so `status`, `task_id`, `context_id`, `message`, `replayed`, `adcp_error`, and the other envelope fields sit beside the body fields at the response root, and `status` is required. Before, 47 of the 124 response and status schemas did not compose it, and 18 closed roots rejected envelope fields outright, so generated SDK types could not represent a conforming response. The 18 are reopened. The async status payloads and the governance responses that omitted `status` now require it. A task-level `message` has one bound of 2,000 characters in `core/task-message.json`, and a seller whose text is longer MUST truncate it rather than fail the task. No body field is added, renamed, or removed.

## New in beta.1 and beta.2

These shipped before beta.3 and are summarized here with the rest. Everything marked experimental is a registered feature id; a seller that emits it lists the id in `experimental_features`.

- **A principal is the bound buyer agent ([#8443](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8443), beta.2).** A principal is the buyer agent as the seller has bound it, and nothing else. `principal_kind` on `get_principal` and `sync_principal` responses, `expected_principal_kind` on `sync_principal` requests, and `enums/principal-kind.json` are deprecated and keep working through 3.x, because a bound buyer agent is always `buyer_agent`.
- **Webhook signing mode is the buyer's choice ([#8475](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8475), beta.1).** The seller signs with the profile the registration's `signing_profile` names, or `adcp/webhook-signing/v1` when it is omitted. A buyer that needs legacy signing sends the deprecated `authentication` block. A seller with `legacy_hmac_fallback: false` rejects that block with `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE`. Condition (b) of `request_signature_required` is deprecated with the block, and a JSON-RPC batch body is rejected with `request_body_malformed` ([#8064](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8064)).
- **Capabilities are bounded by structure ([#8505](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8505), [#8526](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8526)).** Every array, string, and keyed map in `get_adcp_capabilities` has a limit, enforced on new and experimental fields and annotated for 4.0 on fields that were stable before 3.3. Those bounds are the protocol's limit, and agents MUST NOT design to a byte figure. `MAX_CAPABILITIES_BYTES` (64 KiB) is a receiver backstop and deployment guidance, not a protocol rule on senders.
- **Undisclosed rosters ([#7948](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/7948), beta.1).** A product's `publisher_properties` or `collections` can be `[]`, which means the seller does not disclose the roster. There is no new field and no partial state. Allow lists may be accepted against it and cover only what the seller matches. Exclusion lists are the buyer's choice, because the buyer sees the undisclosed roster at discovery: a seller that accepts one applies it to its full internal roster, and a seller that cannot rejects it with `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE`. A seller serving a release below 3.3 never returns such a product. Buyers treat the roster as opaque and may refuse the product. See [Roster coverage](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/media-products#roster-coverage).
- **Canceled and completed buys keep their packages ([#8467](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8467), beta.1).** From 3.3 a seller retains packages on terminal buys, with stable identities, platform mappings, and the latest committed configuration, and discloses irrecoverable history through nonfatal errors instead of returning an incomplete list.
- **Mixed creative approval states at startup ([#8485](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8485), beta.1).** A seller keeps pending-review and rejected assignments and starts the approved ones by default. The optional package `start_requires_all_creatives_approved` gate holds the start until every assignment is approved, and `media_buy.features.creative_start_policy` declares support.
- **Review and escalation commitments ([#8417](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8417), beta.1).** Products can declare creative-review timing and business-clock SLA windows with seller calendars. `report_media_buy_issue` and `get_media_buy_issue` give a human acknowledgement and resolution status for a critical report.
- **Seller offers (experimental, `media_buy.seller_offers`, [#8102](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8102), beta.2).** A seller can propose a change to an accepted buy: a postponed or canceled installment, a preemption, under-delivery. The offer is a `committed` canonical proposal with `proposal_initiator: "seller"` and an `offer_reason`, read through `list_proposals` and announced by the identifiers-only `proposal.offered` notification. The buyer accepts, declines, or revises it with the existing tasks. At `expires_at` the signed terms stand, and silence is never acceptance. Sellers never send offers to callers below 3.3. See [Seller offers](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/proposal-negotiation#seller-offers).
- **Price adjustments (experimental, `media_buy.price_adjustments`, [#7955](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/7955), beta.1).** A fixed-price pricing option can publish an ordered table of fee and discount rows keyed by format option, placement selection, or always, so a buyer computes the price of what it selects. The table is optional, and its resolution order and rounding are normative. See [Selection-dependent price adjustments](/docs/media-buy/advanced-topics/pricing-models#selection-dependent-price-adjustments).
- **Execution readiness (experimental, `media_buy.execution_readiness`, [#7934](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/7934), beta.1).** A `get_products` request with `account` can set `include_execution_readiness: true` to learn, per product and per `execution_requirements` entry, whether the account is `ready`, `setup_required`, `selection_required`, `input_required`, or `unknown`, with candidates whose `resource_id` fits the existing binding field. See [Per-account execution readiness](/docs/media-buy/specification#per-account-execution-readiness-experimental).
- **Signals (experimental).** Signal pricing groups (`signals.pricing_groups`, [#8058](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8058)) let a seller price alternative age bands, for example, as one audience: `pricing_group` on a listing and `signal_pricing_group_rules[]` choose `highest` or `each`. See [Signal pricing groups](/docs/media-buy/advanced-topics/targeting#signal-pricing-groups). Provider-initiated delivery (`signals.delivery_methods`, [#8044](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8044)) lets a signal agent declare the methods it can send over, a buyer pin one with `delivery_method`, and agent deployments report `delivery_status`. See [Declaring delivery methods](/docs/signals/tasks/activate_signal#declaring-delivery-methods).
- **Gender targeting (experimental, `media_buy.gender_targeting`, [#7925](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/7925), beta.1).** Core `demographics.gender` targeting with female, male, and non_binary values, an independent unknown group, product-scoped capabilities, and exact readback for gender-only and mixed age and gender targeting. 3.3 reports demographic delivery by age only, so a gender-targeted package has no gendered delivery readback. Buyers never send core gender fields on a 3.2 contract. See [Core gender targeting](/docs/media-buy/advanced-topics/targeting#core-gender-targeting-3-3).
- **Measurement provenance (experimental, `media_buy.measurement_provenance`, [#7940](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/7940), beta.2).** A shared `collection-mechanism` vocabulary (`set_top_box`, `acr`, `panel`, `server_logs`, `client_tracker`, `sdk`) says what a count is built from: `billing_measurement.counting_mechanisms[]` on `measurement_terms`, and `vendor_metric_provenance[]` on delivery metrics. Sellers serving an older pin omit both.
- **Audience evidence targets (experimental, `media_buy.audience_evidence_targets`, [#8215](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8215), beta.1).** `target_audiences[]` names the audience a buyer wants evidence for, beside `audience_evidence_requirements`. A product satisfies a target only through an admissible evidence item for exactly that audience. A target never targets delivery. See [Target audiences](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products#target-audiences-experimental).
- **Invoice lines (experimental, `account.invoice_lines`, [#8207](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8207), beta.1).** `get_account_financials` invoices can carry `lines[]` that tie an amount to media buys, packages, and reporting revisions, plus `invoice_kind` (`credit_note` carries a non-negative `amount`) and `adjusts_invoice_id`. Lines are reference only and never settle an invoice. See [Invoice lines](/docs/accounts/tasks/get_account_financials#invoice-lines-experimental).
- **Smaller additions.** Exact pre-booking availability checks through structured package inputs on product-scoped `get_products` refinement ([#8468](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8468)); an optional display `name` on measurement catalog metrics ([#8437](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8437)); an optional `metric_id` on performance standards ([#8436](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8436)); one delegation-chain rule for demand-side grants ([#8473](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8473)).

**Version negotiation and closed schemas.** A request that carries only `adcp_major_version` is served the oldest release the seller supports in that major, 3.0 where it is supported ([DR-0034](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/governance/decisions/DR-0034-major-only-requests-are-served-the-oldest-release.md), accepted 2026-10-26). In practice that caller is a 3.0 buyer, so it is no longer answered in a shape its validator rejects. A buyer that wants newer behavior sends `adcp_version`. A schema that a stable release published closed stays closed until the next major: a minor adds only to open schemas, and the 3.2 proposal-flow schemas that were closed against DR-0019 are reopened as a recorded correction ([#8531](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8531), [DR-0036](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/governance/decisions/DR-0036-closed-schemas-stay-closed-within-a-major.md), proposed and awaiting ratification).

## Scheduled and human-gated buying

- **An account is its account ID.** Sellers MUST return `account_id` from `sync_accounts` once the account is resolved, accept it on every account-scoped task, and never change it. A brand is its domain, plus `brand_id` where its `brand.json` lists several brands. Countries, currency, timezone, sandbox, and `operator_unit` are account attributes that a settings update changes without changing the account. Currency, timezone, and sandbox are set at opening, and a seller can review a countries change before applying it. A seller SHOULD return the existing account when a provisioning entry matches exactly one. Natural keys keep working throughout 3.x, but using them as the reference after provisioning is deprecated and planned for removal in 4.0. They remain the way to open an account and to look up its ID. `account.identity_updates`, `ACCOUNT_MOVED`, `ACCOUNT_IDENTITY_CONFLICT`, and `account.stable_account_id` are deprecated. See [Account identity](/docs/accounts/overview#account-identity).
- **Expected confirmation time.** The `submitted` envelope of `create_media_buy`, `buy_products`, and `accept_proposal` can carry `expected_confirmation_at`. A seller whose confirmation waits on a booking round or a manual review window SHOULD set it, and buyers SHOULD check status at that time instead of polling tightly. It is an estimate, not a commitment. `refine_proposals` can carry it too, on a `submitted` finalize whose hold waits on a person's agreement, such as a creator or host. On `buy_products`, `accept_proposal`, and `refine_proposals`, send it only to callers negotiating 3.3. See [Expected confirmation time](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/create_media_buy#expected-confirmation-time).
- **Vendor-metric outcome goals.** `criteria.outcome_target.goal` accepts `kind: "vendor_metric"`, so a buyer can plan "5,000 store visits measured by a foot-traffic vendor", optionally with a cost per visit. The seller answers in each forecast point's `vendor_metric_values[]` and plans only `(vendor, metric_id)` pairs its products declare. Send it only on a negotiated 3.3 version. See [Vendor-metric goals](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/request_proposals#vendor-metric-goals).
- **Governance budget periods (experimental).** A campaign governance plan can split its budget by time with `budget.periods[]`. `check_governance` denies a dated action that straddles a period, sits in unallocated budget, or overruns its period. Governance agents declare `governance.supports_budget_periods`. Part of the experimental `governance.campaign` cluster. See [Budget periods](/docs/governance/campaign/tasks/sync_plans#budget-periods).
- **Holds you can plan and extend (experimental).** Products and proposals can carry a `hold_policy` (`media_buy.hold_policy`), and `refine_proposals` `extend_hold` lengthens a committed hold without re-finalizing (`media_buy.hold_extension`). See [Hold policy](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/refine_proposals#hold-policy-experimental).
- **Delegated composition (experimental).** A platform that owns the catalog can make `product_ids` the complete composable universe with `criteria.product_ids_exclusive`, and a seller that holds no inventory can declare `terms_only` finalize (`media_buy.delegated_composition`). See [delegated composition](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/delegated-composition).
- **Fixed-price, non-guaranteed sellers.** The new `sales-fixed-rate` specialism covers sellers in any medium, digital or linear TV, that publish a fixed price, reserve no inventory, make no delivery commitment, and may hold a buy for review. `get_media_buys` can carry `expected_confirmation_at` while a buy is provisional (`confirmed_at: null`). See [Choosing a sales specialism](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#choosing-a-sales-specialism).
- **Renewal and replacement lineage (experimental).** A new buy can name the buy it succeeds with `predecessor_media_buy_id` and `lineage_reason` (`renewal`, `terms_change`, `migration`, `sunset`, `replacement`). The link is forward-only and same-account; the predecessor is never mutated. Feature id `media_buy.lineage`. See [Media Buy Lineage](/docs/media-buy/specification#media-buy-lineage).
- **Did a failed mutation apply?** `core/error.json` can carry `mutation_outcome` (`not_applied`, `unknown`, `applied`), so a buyer knows whether to re-plan after a failed state-changing request. See [Mutation outcome](/docs/building/by-layer/L3/error-handling#mutation-outcome).
- **Operator fees.** The `operator_fee` price adjustment kind lets an agency's buying platform show a fee it keeps out of the advertiser's budget without changing the seller's price. Sellers never author it. See [Operator fees](/docs/media-buy/advanced-topics/pricing-models#operator-fees).
- **Change cutoff.** Installment deadlines can carry `change_cutoff`, the last moment a creative swap, pause, or stop still takes effect (traffic log close, posting-cycle lock, print issue close). See [Installment deadlines](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/collections-and-installments#installment-deadlines).

## Delivery data offline sellers actually have

- **Spot lifecycle, makegoods, and break position (experimental).** `by_spot[]` rows can carry `spot_status` (`scheduled`, `aired`, `preempted`, `makegood`), `scheduled_at`, `replaces_spot_id`, and `break_position`. Sellers declare what they report, so a missing row is not read as non-delivery, and they never send `scheduled` or `preempted` rows, or the new capability fields, to callers pinned below 3.3. Feature id `media_buy.spot_lifecycle`. See [Spot lifecycle](/docs/media-buy/media-buys/optimization-reporting#spot-lifecycle-makegoods-and-break-position).
- **Print proof of insertion and circulation (experimental).** `print_metrics` on `by_installment` rows reports the page and position delivered, circulation and its basis (`audited` or `seller_modeled`), and a tearsheet `evidence` record. See [Delivery evidence and circulation](/docs/creative/channels/print#delivery-evidence-and-circulation).
- **Property-grain rollups.** `media_buy.features.supports_property_breakdown` and `supports_installment_property_breakdown` commit a seller to return `by_property` and `by_installment_property` on every product that declares the per-product flag. Two capability-gated scenarios grade it. See [`get_adcp_capabilities`](/docs/protocol/get_adcp_capabilities).
- **Structured DOOH location.** Concrete screens can carry `dooh_placement_attributes.location` (`lat`, `lon`, structured `address`), and network products can carry `dooh_inventory_summary.venue_counts[]`. Both are disclosure metadata; `location` never changes placement identity. See [DOOH](/docs/creative/channels/dooh#structured-location-for-planning-and-mapping).
- **Rows without impressions.** Delivery rows may omit `impressions` when the seller did not measure them, for example on rows that report plays or downloads instead. Absence means not measured, never zero; receivers do not derive impression-based rates, and sellers never omit it toward callers pinned below 3.3. See [Rows without impressions](/docs/media-buy/media-buys/optimization-reporting#rows-without-impressions).
- **Delivery projection (experimental).** Top-level `by_package[]` delivery rows can carry an advisory `projection` of lifetime impressions and spend with a `projected_end_state` and `basis`. It is a forecast, not a delivery record. Feature id `media_buy.delivery_projection`. See [Delivery projection](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buy_delivery#delivery-projection).
- **Installment lifecycle and proof of publication (experimental).** `by_installment[]` rows can carry `installment_status` and a `publication` record (`published_at`, `posts[]`, optional `evidence`), so a creator post, newsletter edition, or podcast episode reports whether and where it ran. Feature id `media_buy.installment_lifecycle`. See [Installment lifecycle](/docs/media-buy/media-buys/optimization-reporting#installment-lifecycle-and-proof-of-publication).
- **Report source custody.** A Reliable Reporting report definition's `source` can say whether the producer is the `system_of_record` or derived its figures `upstream_derived`.
- **Audience sizes as ranges.** `sync_audiences` results can carry `audience_size` and `targetable_size` as bounded ranges with a `precision`, plus `match_rate_basis`. `matched_count` and `effective_match_rate` may now be absent, and buyers MUST NOT read absence as zero. See [Audience size](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/sync_audiences#audience-size).

## Creator and collection cards (experimental)

Collections can carry images, sample content, and follower counts, talent can link to its brand identity, and product and proposal responses can return full collection objects on request (`media_buy.collection_cards`, `brand.collection_links`). See [Collection cards](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/collections-and-installments#collection-cards-experimental).

## Open opportunities and later proposals (experimental)

A buyer can keep an opportunity open after the first response and let sellers send more. `opportunity.later_proposals` gives consent with optional `until`, `max_count`, and `min_interval` limits. Sellers then create draft-only later proposals, announce each with the identifiers-only `proposal.created` notification, and serve them through the new [`list_proposals`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/list_proposals) read, which returns each snapshot beside its disposition. `accept_proposal` and `buy_products` accept an explicit `opportunity.status: "open"` so a buyer can buy part of a plan and keep sourcing. Everything is experimental under `media_buy.open_opportunities` and advertised in parts. See [Open opportunities and later proposals](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/proposal-negotiation#open-opportunities-and-later-proposals) and [#8096](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/8096).

## Agent identity and buyer self-onboarding (experimental)

A buyer agent can now make itself known to a seller it has never spoken to. The new Web Bot Auth signing profile identifies the signer by its origin and verifies against that origin's key directory, with no capabilities call and no seller-issued credential. The operator's trust.json lists its agents; brands publish grants in theirs, and sellers publish their onboarding posture in `account.agent_onboarding`. `get_principal` reports relationship status and expiry. The RFC 9421 profile is deprecated in 3.3 and works through 3.x. See [A buyer agent onboards itself](/docs/accounts/buyer-agent-self-onboarding) and the [agent identity migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/agent-identity-3-3).

**Account requests (experimental, beta.1).** Where a seller supports `account.requests`, `request_account` is the one account write. Send `brand` and `operator` to open an account, or an `account_id` with the attributes to change (the brand's `countries`, `operator_unit`, `billing`, `billing_entity`, `payment_terms`). Each write is a request that either resolves in the same response or goes `in_review` with one pending-action shape saying whose turn it is. A `revision` guards the change, and it is required when the change touches money. `list_accounts` reads open requests with `include_requests` and pages them separately from accounts through `requests_pagination`. The unreleased `get_account_requests` task is gone. `sync_accounts` stays stable everywhere: where `account.requests` is supported, buyers SHOULD use `request_account`, `list_accounts`, and `sync_principal` instead, and an entry carrying only subscription fields never provisions or reviews an account. In beta.2, a change request can also resolve to an account the caller already holds (`matched`) or, when the buyer sets `allow_separate_account`, to a new account carrying the change (`created`), leaving the named account untouched. A seller that would only accept the change on a new account otherwise refuses with `ACCOUNT_CHANGE_REQUIRES_NEW_ACCOUNT`. A `sync_accounts` settings update under review carries its `request_id`, so a refusal is visible. Two accounts that end up alike after a change are not a conflict, and `countries` decide products, prices, terms and reviews, never targeting; narrowing them never changes an accepted buy. See [`request_account`](/docs/accounts/tasks/request_account).

`sync_event_sources` and `sync_audiences` results now use optional `platform_id` for the object's ID in the seller's ad platform, matching creatives and catalogs. ## Installments on the compact product lifecycle

For scheduled media the installment *is* the thing being sold — a slot in a creator's upcoming video, a podcast episode, a sponsored newsletter issue. The canonical Product that `list_products` and the proposal lifecycle read had no `installments`, so a proposal could name the creator but not which slot, when it publishes, or its material deadlines. 3.3 adds `installments[]` of the new `core/canonical-installment.json`, whose identity is the `(collection_ref, installment_id)` tuple alone. Request it in `fields`; omitting it on a product that declares `collections` still means run-of-collection.

A purchase buys the product as offered. Naming the exact slots a purchase buys, and dropping or swapping one through `refine_proposals`, was merged for 3.3 and is withdrawn to 4.0 ([below](#withdrawn-from-3-3-planned-for-4-0)). See [Installments in the compact lifecycle](/docs/media-buy/product-discovery/collections-and-installments#installments-in-the-compact-lifecycle).

## Tightened rules

These narrow what a conformant implementation may do. Each is detailed in the [migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#required-to-claim-3-3-behavior) and recorded in [Versioning and governance](/docs/reference/versioning#3-3-exceptions-to-3-x-compatibility-beta).

- **Discovery never provisions an account.** Discovery and negotiation tasks MUST NOT create or activate an account or accept default terms. See [Account references before provisioning](/docs/accounts/overview#account-references-before-provisioning).
- **One agent-resolution rule.** Request signing, webhook signing, governance JWS `iss`, designated-task response signing, and rights attestations share one algorithm for which brand.json lists an agent and which keys it may sign with. Some signatures that verified under 3.2 now fail. See [Agent resolution](/docs/building/by-layer/L1/security#agent-resolution).
- **`required_for` over A2A, hardened.** The A2A operation-resolution errata shipped in 3.2.3. Its hardening clauses were SHOULD in 3.2.x and are MUST from 3.3. Verifiers declare support with the new optional `request_signing.operation_sources` capability; a counterparty MUST NOT infer A2A enforcement from an absent field, and buyers sign every operation in `required_for` regardless of the marker. See [Operation resolution over A2A](/docs/building/by-layer/L1/security#operation-resolution-over-a2a).
- **`reporting_webhook.operation_id` fails closed.** The seller echoes it into every `media_buy_delivery` webhook and sends none to a registration that omits it. See [Reporting webhooks](/docs/building/by-layer/L3/webhooks#reporting-webhooks).
- **Advertise only the measurement terms you accept.** See [Advertise only what you accept](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products#advertise-only-what-you-accept).
- **Audience identifiers keep their wire form.** `uids[].value` for `uid2` and `euid` is the raw identifier, never the encrypted token, and is never case-folded or re-encoded.
- **brand.json fetch limits.** The cap rises to 2 MiB, with new MUSTs for streaming enforcement, nesting depth, and bounded fetches.
- **Delivery rows may omit `impressions`** toward 3.3 callers only, and receivers MUST NOT read absence as zero or derive impression-denominated values. The schema loosens; the rules around it narrow.
- **Receivers ignore `aggregated_totals` in delivery webhooks** and do not reject a payload for carrying it.
- **Counterparty-asserted domain lists are bounded.** `media_buy.portfolio.publisher_domains` and `signals.data_provider_domains` have a normative byte budget, with completeness flags for truncation; buyers never read absence from either list as a denial. See [below](#nobody-else-gets-to-write-your-capabilities-document).
- **Platform IDs under both names.** A seller reporting an event source or audience platform ID emits `platform_id` and the deprecated `seller_id` alias with identical values. See [Platform object IDs have one name](#platform-object-ids-have-one-name).
- **Property domain identifiers have an enforceable format.** `domain` and `subdomain` values must be lowercase ASCII hostnames; mixed-case, trailing-dot, and URL-shaped values are rejected. See [the migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#property-domain-identifiers-have-an-enforceable-format).
- **Property list references resolve through AdCP tasks.** Issuers expose `get_property_list` over MCP and accept the reference's list-scoped Bearer token; a custom download route alone no longer suffices. See [the migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#property-list-references-resolve-through-adcp-tasks).
- **Flight start activates committed buys only.** A provisional buy (`confirmed_at: null`) never auto-activates; it stays `pending_start` until the seller confirms or rejects it. See [Provisional buys](/docs/media-buy/specification#provisional-buys-seller-review-hold).
- **Seller revisions advance on every committed change.** The media-buy `revision` advances on every committed change to the state `get_media_buys` reports, whatever its origin: an AdCP task, a seller operator, seller automation, another authorized principal, a connected platform, or a `sync_creatives` assignment. `history` entries for externally originated changes carry the same `origin.kind` vocabulary as `account.change`, and creatives gain an optional seller-maintained `seller_revision` that `account.change.resource_revision` carries. Buyers that send a stale `revision` will see more `CONFLICT`; that is intended. Recorded as [DR-0031](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/governance/decisions/DR-0031-seller-revision-any-origin.md). See [the migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#seller-revisions-advance-on-every-committed-change).
- **Multi-qualifier metrics omit flat totals.** When a buy's packages report a metric under more than one qualifier set, such as viewability at `mrc` and `groupm`, the seller omits that metric's flat carrier from buy and window `totals`; read the package rows. See [the migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#multi-qualifier-metrics-omit-flat-totals).
- **Refinement carries compliance forward.** A `build_creative` refinement keeps the parent leaf's required disclosures and prohibited claims, and a refined leaf never inherits approval. See [the migration guide](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#refinement-carries-compliance-forward).

Two more guarantees loosen here, and the response-size default a third ([below](#bounded-agent-responses)): a 3.2 verifier can reject a brand.json between 256 KiB and 2 MiB that a 3.3 verifier accepts, and a `ready` audience no longer promises match counts. Separately, `reporting_webhook.authentication` and `artifact_webhook.authentication` become optional on requests served at 3.3, so an integration can drop HMAC before 4.0 ([details](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#reporting-and-artifact-webhooks-can-omit-authentication)). Many new fields sit inside objects 3.2 closes, so send them only to peers that negotiated 3.3; see [Send only to 3.3 peers](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#send-only-to-3-3-peers).

A declaring seller rejects out-of-declaration requests with `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE` before changing state. See [`sync_catalogs`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/sync_catalogs#ingestion-compatibility).

See [AdCP opportunity extension](/docs/building/by-layer/L2/context-sessions#adcp-opportunity-extension).

## Bounded agent responses

A seller agent that answers a discovery call with tens of megabytes is an availability attack on every buyer that reads the body before checking its size. 3.3 adds [Response size bounds](/docs/building/by-layer/L1/security#response-size-bounds): clients SHOULD bound every AdCP response they read, enforce the bound while the body is received rather than after buffering or parsing, cover SSE per event and cumulatively, surface an oversized response as an error rather than truncating it, and bound JSON depth and node count once the byte cap passes — the byte cap bounds ingress, the parse bounds bound memory. The default is 4 MiB, matching what the SDKs and the hosted grader ship, and 4 MiB is the ceiling on what any default may be.

This is a recommendation, not a conformance requirement, but the interop effect is real in both directions. A buyer adopting the default rejects a response it would previously have buffered, and the rejection arrives as a mid-body connection abort rather than an `adcp_error`. A buyer still on the older 1 MiB figure rejects a response a 3.3 buyer accepts.

The seller-side counterpart matters more than the number: keep discovery responses small. `tools/list`, `get_adcp_capabilities`, and the A2A agent card are the first bytes a counterparty reads, and an oversized one makes an agent unreachable rather than slow. `get_adcp_capabilities` is the sharpest case for an agent that signs under the deprecated RFC 9421 profile: above `MAX_CAPABILITIES_BYTES` (64 KiB), that profile's key discovery fails and your signing keys stop resolving too. Outside that path, the structural bounds are the protocol's limit on the capabilities response, and 64 KiB is only a receiver backstop, not a design target. See [Discovery responses stay small](/docs/building/concepts/managing-response-size#discovery-responses-stay-small).

### Bounded inbound push and webhook bodies

The response bound covers what a client reads on its own call path. 3.3 adds the receiver-side counterpart: [Inbound body bounds](/docs/building/by-layer/L1/security#inbound-body-bounds). Receivers of A2A push notifications and AdCP webhooks (reporting, artifact, collection-list, and account notification subscribers) SHOULD pre-reject an over-limit `Content-Length`, count decoded bytes while streaming, apply the 64-deep, 100,000-member parse bounds after the byte cap and before RFC 9421 or Web Bot Auth verification, apply connect, idle, and total deadlines, and reject with HTTP 413 rather than truncating. The default cap is 4 MiB, generalizing the attestation task-body cap rather than adding a new number, and a tighter per-feature cap governs. This is a recommendation, not a conformance requirement.

## Nobody else gets to write your capabilities document

Two capability fields enumerate relationships a *counterparty* asserts:
`media_buy.portfolio.publisher_domains` and `signals.data_provider_domains` are both
populated from those counterparties' `/.well-known/adagents.json`, which any domain
owner publishes unilaterally and without the named agent's consent. An agent that
mirrored every publisher claiming it had handed an unauthenticated stranger a write
primitive into its own discovery document — and since the RFC 9421 signing-key path
fetches that document unnarrowed under `MAX_CAPABILITIES_BYTES`, pushing it past
64 KiB cost a victim that signed under that profile *signature verification*,
arriving a cache cycle later with no visible connection to its cause.

3.3 adds a normative byte budget — 8,192 bytes for any single
counterparty-asserted field, 16,384 bytes for both together — and declares
structural bounds (100 unique entries, 253 characters each) that the conformance
storyboard grades in 3.x and the schema enforces in 4.0. No previously-valid
document becomes schema-invalid; what changes is what counts as conformant. Because a bounded
list would otherwise make a large legitimate network look unauthorized for most of
its own inventory, the bound ships with `publisher_domains_complete` and
`data_provider_domains_complete` — default `true`, set `false` when you truncate.
Both lists are explicitly non-exhaustive, so a buyer must resolve the publisher named
on a product through that publisher's own `adagents.json` and must never read absence
from the list as a denial.

## Account change feed: recognize your own changes

The experimental account change feed (`account.change_feed`) gains two changes ahead of graduation. A record whose `origin.kind` is `adcp` can echo the originating request's `idempotency_key`, so a buyer recognizes its own changes on a shared account; the seller includes it only on records served to the principal that sent the request, and every other reader sees the same record without it. And `changed_paths` is now required when `origin.kind` is `adcp` or `seller_operator`, staying best-effort for `seller_system` and `connected_platform`. See [`list_account_changes`](/docs/accounts/tasks/list_account_changes#recognizing-your-own-changes).

## Reporting subscriptions on the principal (experimental, beta.1 to beta.3)

Reliable Reporting was configured one account at a time, so a new account reported nothing until someone configured it. `sync_principal` now takes `reporting_subscriptions`: one registration per feed purpose, scoped to `all_authorized_accounts: true` or to named `account_ids`, with a selector that works across sellers (`feed_purpose`, `period_duration`, `required_finality`, optional `dimensions`, and `match`). The buyer can work out coverage without asking: a confirmed buy is owed every complete period that starts on or after the later of the subscription version's `accepted_at` and the account's `authorization.authorized_at`, from the period in which the buy is confirmed through its final period. `get_reporting_status` reports only exceptions under `subscription_coverage`. Exceptions remove periods from what is owed and never add any, so a missing report still means an owed period with nothing delivered. Revoked accounts appear in the subscription readback on `get_principal`, not as silent gaps. A caller's own `sync_accounts` delivery config still wins for that account and feed purpose. Sellers list accepted registration tasks in `reporting_delivery.configuration_tasks[]`. `configuration_task` stays `"sync_accounts"` for older clients and is deprecated. In beta.2, offerings publish `standard_dimensions` so a buyer resolves the selector from the wire, and the readback carries `inactive_intervals`, per-version configuration in `prior_versions`, and per-offering gaps and timezone cutovers. Period arithmetic (civil days, elapsed hours), cutovers across different offerings, and authorization chains on re-grant are now pinned. Feature id `media_buy.reporting_subscriptions`. See [Reporting subscriptions](/docs/protocol/sync_principal#reporting-subscriptions) and [Subscription coverage](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_reporting_status#subscription-coverage).

## What requires adopter attention

| If you are… | Review before advertising 3.3 |
|---|---|
| A seller that provisions accounts lazily | Stop provisioning on discovery tasks. |
| A seller with a media-buy, account or creative revision | Advance it on every committed change from any origin, including `sync_creatives` assignments and ingested connected-platform changes; add `origin` to `history` entries. |
| A buyer that passes `revision` on updates | Expect `CONFLICT` after seller, connected-platform or `sync_creatives` changes; re-read and retry with the new `revision`. |
| A signing or verifying implementation | Resolve agents by canonical URL from `identity.brand_json_url`, publish every pinned key in your JWKS, apply the A2A hardening clauses, and raise the brand.json cap to 2 MiB. |
| A seller sending delivery webhooks | Echo `reporting_webhook.operation_id`, and send nothing to a registration without one. |
| A seller returning proposals | Advertise only measurement terms you accept on replay. If you implemented definition pins from `3.3.0-beta.0`, stop emitting `definition_pins[]`, `definition_changed`, and `REFERENCE_DEFINITION_CHANGED` to beta.1 and later peers ([withdrawn](#withdrawn-from-3-3-planned-for-4-0)). |
| A fixed-price, non-guaranteed seller | Claim `sales-fixed-rate`; keep a held buy in `pending_start` until you confirm or reject it. |
| A seller that wants to send proposals after the first response | Opt in to `media_buy.open_opportunities`: advertise `list_proposals` first, then `later_proposals` with a `max_open_duration`, and honor buyer consent and limits. |
| A linear TV, print, or DOOH seller | Adopt the delivery fields you can populate; gate 3.3-only rows and fields on the negotiated version. |
| An SDK maintainer | Generate from the signed `3.3.0-beta.3` tarball, not `main`, and check the [SDK-facing changes](/docs/reference/migration/3-2-to-3-3#sdk-and-code-generator-maintainers). |
| A seller that opens buyer-declared accounts | Return `account_id` from `sync_accounts`, accept it on every account-scoped task, and never change it; update countries and other attributes in place. |
| A seller supporting `account.requests` | Return `account`, with its money terms, on every request that resolves `matched` or `created`; send `effective_at` on money changes and `not_carried_over` on a separate account; refuse `currency` or `timezone` changes you don't list in `changeable_attributes`. |
| A seller advertising `media_buy.reporting_subscriptions` | Publish `cancellation_effective_at`, `flight_history`, per-package `reporting_delivery_offering_ids`, `authorization.regranted_at`, and offering `available_from` / `withdrawn_at`; owe billing stubs at cutovers; deliver only inside a buy's flight; key managed-delivery rows on `account_id`. |
| A buyer that sends natural keys after provisioning | Store the returned `account_id` and send it; natural-key references are deprecated ahead of 4.0. |
| A seller reporting event source or audience platform IDs | Emit `platform_id` and the deprecated `seller_id` alias with identical values, including discovery responses. |
| A buyer consuming event source or audience platform IDs | Prefer `platform_id`, fall back to `seller_id` when absent, and keep using buyer object IDs in AdCP requests. |
| A seller with a catalog intake | Declare `catalog_ingestion` if buyers need your limits, and reject out-of-declaration requests before mutation. |
| A seller advertising `account.change_feed` | Include `changed_paths` on every `adcp` and `seller_operator` record; optionally echo `idempotency_key` on `adcp` records, to the sending principal only. |
| A buyer or SDK maintainer | Adopt the 4 MiB response-size default; size your aggregate budget, not just the per-call one, if you fan out across agents. |
| Any agent operator | Measure `tools/list`, `get_adcp_capabilities`, and your agent card. Oversized discovery makes you unreachable, and oversized capabilities also breaks signing-key discovery. |
| A buyer operating a webhook or push-notification receiver | Bound the request body (4 MiB default) before signature verification, and answer an over-limit request with 413. |
| A seller listing a principal on `get_principal` or `sync_principal` | Keep returning `principal_kind` and honoring `expected_principal_kind` through 3.x; buyers stop reading and sending them. |
| A seller that registers webhooks | Sign with the profile `signing_profile` names, default `adcp/webhook-signing/v1`; with `legacy_hmac_fallback: false`, reject an `authentication` block with `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE`. |
| A seller that returns product rosters | Return `publisher_properties: []` or `collections: []` only to callers served 3.3 or later; apply any exclusion list you accept against your full internal roster. |
| A seller with canceled or completed buys | Keep the packages, with stable identities and the latest committed configuration, and disclose irrecoverable history through nonfatal errors. |
| A seller or SDK that builds task responses | Compose the protocol envelope on every response, include `status`, and truncate (MUST, not fail) a `message` over 2,000 characters. Regenerate types from the beta.3 schemas. |
| An in-house client that sends only `adcp_major_version` | You are served the oldest supported release in that major (3.0 where supported). Send `adcp_version` to get newer behavior. |
| A seller that advertises `get_adcp_capabilities` fields | Stay within the structural bounds; do not design to a byte figure. |
| A seller that wants to offer a change to a signed buy | Opt in to `media_buy.seller_offers` after `media_buy.open_opportunities`; send offers only to callers served 3.3. |
| A seller of signals | Declare `signals.delivery_methods` before accepting a `delivery_method` pin; publish `signal_pricing_group_rules[]` if listings share a `pricing_group`. |
| A seller with live creatives | Declare `creative.live_updates` per change kind, and refuse the rest with `CREATIVE_LIVE_UPDATE_UNSUPPORTED`. |
| A compliance operator | Treat each 3.3 beta bundle as an implementation input. As for 3.2, prerelease grading runs are diagnostic, not a badge. |

## Withdrawn from 3.3, planned for 4.0

Three features are not part of 3.3. All three add terms to `commercial_terms`, and `commercial-terms` stays closed within 3.x: a buyer must not be bound by terms it cannot read, and `terms_digest` signs exactly those terms. If the object were open, a 3.2 buyer could accept a 3.3 term it silently ignored. 4.0 gives `commercial_terms` a declared extension point with must-understand and brings them back ([adcp#8501](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/8501)).

| Feature | Where it stood | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition pins** (`commercial_terms.definition_pins[]`, the `definition_changed` refinement reason, `REFERENCE_DEFINITION_CHANGED`, #7960) | Published in `3.3.0-beta.0` and `3.3.0-beta.1` | Removed after beta.1. Stop emitting pins and the reason and code, and stop requiring them. The `definition_pin_drift` storyboard and the controller `definition_pin_probe` scenario are removed with them. |
| **Purchase rights** (`purchases[].rights[]`, `media_buy.purchase_rights`, #8101) | Published in `3.3.0-beta.1` (experimental) | Removed after beta.1, together with the `brand/rights-scope.json` hoist and the `paid_amplification` and `content_reuse` values of `right-use`. `brand/rights-terms.json` returns to its 3.2 form. |
| **Installment selection** (`purchases[].installments[]`, `refine_proposals` `installment_changes`, `media_buy.installment_selection`, #8246) | Published in `3.3.0-beta.1` (experimental) | Removed after beta.1. Installments on the canonical product and `holds[]` stay in 3.3. |

The designs are kept for 4.0: see [Planned for 4.0](/docs/reference/migration/v4-compatibility#planned-for-4-0-withdrawn-from-3-3).

## In review for 3.3

Open 3.3 proposals are not part of any beta until they merge. Five working-group decisions are ratified or accepted, and their normative text has merged:

- **Agent identity is an origin ([DR-0023](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/governance/decisions/DR-0023-agent-identity-is-an-origin.md)).** Merged: the [Web Bot Auth profile](/docs/building/by-layer/L1/wba-profile) [#7894](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/7894), Web Bot Auth identities and the deprecation of the RFC 9421 brand.json profile [#8162](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8162), first-contact binding [#8168](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8168), the buyer walkthrough and migration guide [#8172](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8172), the experimental production security profile [#8155](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8155), seller onboarding posture [#8170](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8170), and the R7/R8 erratum [#8219](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8219). `request_account` [#8293](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8293), then the single account write and reporting subscriptions ([#8543](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/8543): [#8551](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8551), [#8552](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8552)).
- **Responses conform to the served release ([DR-0024](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/governance/decisions/DR-0024-responses-conform-to-served-release.md), [#8117](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/pull/8117)).** Merged.
