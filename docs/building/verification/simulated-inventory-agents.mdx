<Note>
**Non-normative.** These are sandbox tenants of the public test agent, built for testing buyer and storefront agents over the life of a buy. The `sandbox_clock` controller scenario is proposed in [#9025](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/9025) and is served only by these two tenants for now. Their behaviour is a simulation, not a model of any real ad server.
</Note>

Most test agents answer each call correctly but hold fixed data. Nothing forecasts from traffic, serves a booking day by day, or delivers differently from what was forecast. The two inventory agents on the test agent do all three, driven by a seeded simulation and a clock you can move. You can test what your agent does over a 30-day flight in a few calls: when a guarantee falls behind, when actual traffic differs from the forecast, or when an episode is cancelled.

Both play the *inventory* role from [#8993](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/8993): they hold the catalogue and avails, execute media buys and report delivery. A storefront agent can book through both, as their buyer, over AdCP.

## Connect

| Tenant | URL | Sells |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Ad server | `https://test-agent.adcontextprotocol.org/inventory-adserver/mcp` | Display, pre-roll video and a homepage takeover on two sites, guaranteed and in a first-price auction |
| Content platform | `https://test-agent.adcontextprotocol.org/inventory-content/mcp` | Exclusive sponsorships of a weekly podcast, a weekly newsletter and a fortnightly creator series |

Authenticate with the same bearer tokens as the rest of the test agent; see the [quickstart](/docs/quickstart). Each tenant serves the media buy and creative tasks as they are, with no new tasks:

- [`get_products`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products) returns each product with a `forecast` for the flight in `filters.start_date` and `filters.end_date` (30 days from now when omitted). `filters.countries` narrows the forecast. `filters.delivery_type` and `filters.channels` filter the products. Both `brief` and `wholesale` buying modes are served, so a storefront can read either tenant as a wholesale catalogue.
- [`create_media_buy`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/create_media_buy) books each package:
  - Guaranteed packages take `impressions` as their goal. Without it, the goal is what the budget buys at the fixed price.
  - The takeover and the sponsorships take every impression of what they cover.
  - Auction packages bid `bid_price`, first-price, above the floor.
  - `targeting_overlay.geo_countries` (`US`, `CA`) narrows a package.
  - A package's `creatives` (uploaded inline) and `creative_assignments` (from your library) are assigned to it.
- [`update_media_buy`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/update_media_buy) pauses, resumes and cancels a buy or a package, and changes a package's `budget`, `impressions` or `bid_price`, or the buy's `end_time`. A package's `creatives` or `creative_assignments` replace its assignments.
- [`get_media_buys`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buys) and [`get_media_buy_delivery`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buy_delivery) report what was booked and what was delivered. They include `by_package`, `daily_breakdown` and `pacing_index` for guaranteed packages. In `get_media_buys`, each package lists its `creative_approvals`.
- [`sync_creatives`](/docs/creative/task-reference/sync_creatives), [`list_creatives`](/docs/creative/task-reference/list_creatives) and [`list_creative_formats`](/docs/creative/task-reference/list_creative_formats) manage the creatives you traffic your buys with. See [Creatives](#creatives).

Everything you book is private to your credential. All the accounts you use with one credential book into the same simulated market on each tenant, so your own buys compete for inventory. A `get_products` call that names an `account` returns `cache_scope: "account"`, with forecasts net of your bookings. One without an `account` returns the public card, `cache_scope: "public"`, with forecasts against an empty market.

Neither tenant requires operator authentication, so a storefront can call them with its own credential. The publishers use `.example` domains, so a registry or DNS check of their properties fails. That is expected in this sandbox.

## Creatives

Both tenants keep a creative library for each account. Sync creatives to it with [`sync_creatives`](/docs/creative/task-reference/sync_creatives), or upload them inline on a package, and assign them to the packages you have bought.

- **Formats.** [`list_creative_formats`](/docs/creative/task-reference/list_creative_formats) returns only the formats the tenant serves: display, pre-roll video and the takeover image on the ad server; the podcast spot, newsletter sponsorship and creator brief on the content platform. Name a creative's format canonically, with `format_kind` and the `params` of one of the product's `format_options` (or its `format_option_ref`); the size of the creative's image or video picks the format when `params` is omitted. A named `format_id` copied from `format_options` also works. A request whose format still matches more than one option is refused, naming them. A creative can only be assigned to a package whose product serves its format. Otherwise the request fails with [`FORMAT_NOT_SUPPORTED`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-format-not-supported).
- **Assignments.** `assignment_operations` supports `assign`, `unassign` and `replace`. A request's operations apply together or not at all. The deprecated `assignments` field still works, and reports each failed assignment in the creative's `assignment_errors`. Merges, assignment-level trackers and landing pages, installment scoping and placement routing are rejected with [`UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-unsupported-feature).
- **Review and delivery.** Every creative is approved as soon as it arrives. A buy reports `pending_creatives` while none of its live packages has a creative, then `pending_start` or `active` as usual. Delivery does not wait for a creative, though: an unassigned package delivers exactly as the simulation forecast. The creatives exist to test trafficking, not to gate serving.
- **Scope.** Creatives are private to the account that synced them, like the buys they are assigned to. Each account holds up to 200 creatives of up to 32 KiB each; reference hosted media by URL. `reset_state` clears them with the rest of your market.

## Move the clock

Every time-dependent behaviour of these tenants reads your *sandbox clock*. Until you move it, it reads the wall clock. Move it with [`comply_test_controller`](/docs/building/by-layer/L3/comply-test-controller):

```json
{ "scenario": "sandbox_clock", "params": { "operation": "get" } }
```

```json
{ "scenario": "sandbox_clock", "params": { "operation": "advance_time", "to": "2026-12-31T12:00:00Z" } }
```

```json
{ "scenario": "sandbox_clock", "params": { "operation": "advance_time", "by": "P30D" } }
```

- `get` returns `simulated.simulated_now`, and `pinned: false` while the clock still reads the wall clock.
- `advance_time` takes either `to` (an RFC 3339 instant) or `by` (an ISO 8601 duration in weeks, days, hours, minutes or seconds). It returns the new `simulated_now` and `days_crossed`, the number of UTC days the clock moved across. The tenant you call runs those days at once; the other runs them on your next call to it, with the same results.
- The clock only moves forward. An earlier instant is refused with `INVALID_TRANSITION`.
- `reset_state` on either inventory tenant unpins the clock and discards your buys on both. A `reset_state` elsewhere that unpins the clock, such as `/sales/mcp`'s, also leaves your inventory buys behind: a market that has run days the clock has not reached starts fresh.
- You have one clock per credential. It is the same clock that `reporting_subscription_probe` `advance_time` moves on `/sales/mcp`, so moving it on either tenant moves it for both.

Both tenants list `sandbox_clock` and `reset_state` in `list_scenarios` and in `compliance_testing.scenarios`.

## What happens each simulated day

When the clock passes the end of a day, that day runs:

1. **Bookings start.** A buy confirmed by the end of day N serves from day N+1, or from its flight start if that is later.
2. **Actual traffic arrives.** It differs from the forecast by seeded noise and by the season's scripted events.
3. **Guarantees are served first.** The takeover and the sponsorships take their share. Impression goals are then served by the high-water mark method. Each package's daily target is its remaining goal divided by its remaining days. The package with the least usable supply goes first, and each takes the same share of every impression it can use, never more than its target. A package that falls short spreads the shortfall over its remaining days.
4. **The auction takes what is left.** The highest bid above the floor wins until each package's daily budget is spent.
5. **Installments run.** Each one runs, is postponed or is cancelled, as the season says. One that runs builds its audience over its channel's curve: newsletter opens over 3 days, podcast listens over 30, video views over 6 weeks.

Delivery is reported once the day has closed and the product's reporting delay has passed: the next day for the websites, a day later for the podcast and the creator series. A sponsorship package keeps delivering after its flight ends, while its installments' audience builds.

## The season

The simulation is deterministic: the same calls at the same simulated times always give the same results. The season runs from 2026-11-01 to 2028-10-30 and includes these scripted events:

| Tenant | When | Event |
|--------|------|-------|
| Ad server | 2027-01-08 to 2027-01-12 | The Summit Sports hero unit serves a quarter of its traffic. A guarantee on `sports_hero_guaranteed` falls behind, then catches up. |
| Ad server | 2027-01-20 to 2027-01-22 | Pinnacle News traffic nearly doubles. |
| Content | 2027-01-18 | The Morning Brief episode `morning_brief_012` is cancelled. Its sponsor receives nothing for it. |
| Content | 2027-01-18 | The Maker Studio video `maker_studio_006` is postponed to 2027-01-23. |

A content product lists the installments scheduled in the requested flight, with each one's `status` as known at your sandbox time: `scheduled`, then `published`, `postponed` (with the new `scheduled_at`) or `cancelled` once its date passes. A sponsorship package buys every installment of its product scheduled in its flight.

## A month in five calls

On `/inventory-adserver/mcp`:

1. Pin the clock before the flight: `sandbox_clock` `advance_time` `to` `2026-12-31T12:00:00Z`.
2. [`create_media_buy`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/create_media_buy) for 2027-01-01 to 2027-01-31 with one package on `sports_hero_guaranteed` (`pricing_option_id` `sports_hero_guaranteed_cpm`, `impressions` 180000).
3. `create_media_buy` for the same flight with one package on `preroll_auction` (`preroll_auction_cpm`, `bid_price` 10). Two buys keep each one's `daily_breakdown` separate.
4. `sandbox_clock` `advance_time` `by` `P31D`.
5. [`get_media_buy_delivery`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buy_delivery) for both buys.

In the daily breakdown, the guarantee drops to under half its usual delivery during the outage and delivers above its earlier pace afterwards. The auction package's delivery differs from the forecast [`get_products`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products) gave for the same flight.

## Run on your own season

The two tenants run on any season you supply as a file, so you can test against your own sites, products and scripted events. Run the training agent on your machine and point a tenant at the file. The hosted test agent serves only its shipped seasons, so a season file never leaves your machine.

A season is a YAML or JSON file. Its fields are the `SeasonSeed` type in [`engine/types.ts`](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/types.ts). The starter, `server/tests/fixtures/inventory-sim/small-season.yaml`, is a complete season for one site with a guaranteed and an auction product. Copy it and edit it. The sketch below shows the shape only. It does not load on its own, because it leaves out required fields such as `audience_profiles`, `segments`, `traffic` and `forecast_error`. The starter file is the complete, loadable example.

```yaml
seed_id: small-season
rng_seed: small-season-v1
start_date: 2026-11-01
days: 365
currency: USD
regions:
  - { region_id: us, country: US, name: United States, share: 1 }
properties:
  - { property_id: harbor_daily, name: Harbor Daily, domain: harbor-daily.example }
placements:
  - placement_id: banner
    channel: display
    property_ids: [harbor_daily]
    base_daily_impressions: 100000
    floor_cpm: 2
products:
  - product_id: banner_guaranteed
    delivery_type: guaranteed
    placement_ids: [banner]
    rate_cpm: 8
```

The file is checked against the `SeasonSeed` shape and its value ranges (rates are between 0 and 1, lags, cadences and counts are whole numbers, sigmas and multipliers are not negative), and then against the engine's own rules. The days are an integer from 1 to 1100, and `weekday_shape` has seven values. Region and audience-profile shares are positive and sum to 1. The currency is an upper-case three-letter code, property domains are lower-case hostnames, floors are not negative, and every placement lists at least one format. Ids are unique and every reference resolves. Each placement on the `display` or `olv` channel has `base_daily_impressions` (podcast, newsletter and creator-video placements take their audience from their collections instead), and each guaranteed product has `rate_cpm`. Placement `formats` must be format labels the training agent knows, such as `display_300x250` or `video_preroll`; the error for any other lists them.

Set one environment variable per tenant:

| Tenant | Variable |
|--------|----------|
| Ad server | `TRAINING_AGENT_INVENTORY_ADSERVER_SEASON_FILE` |
| Content platform | `TRAINING_AGENT_INVENTORY_CONTENT_SEASON_FILE` |

A tenant whose variable is unset or empty serves its shipped season. A tenant whose variable is set serves the file, and logs one line naming it. If the file is missing or invalid, the server refuses to start. The error names the variable and the path. The shape check lists every problem it finds, and the engine's rules report the first.

Change `seed_id` whenever you change a season file. A market already stored under an unchanged `seed_id` would replay under the new traffic.

The server needs PostgreSQL. Start the one in `docker-compose.yml`, create the schema the migrations expect, then start the server from the repository root. The one-line `create schema` is enough for a throwaway local database. The [local bootstrap](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/runbooks/training-agent-gcs-reporting.md#local-bootstrap) in the runbook has the full setup.

```bash
docker compose up -d --wait postgres
docker compose exec -T postgres psql -U adcp -d adcp_registry \
  -c "create schema if not exists training_reporting_gcs authorization adcp"

PORT=3917 RUN_MIGRATIONS=true FLY_PROCESS_GROUP=web \
  DATABASE_URL="postgresql://adcp:localdev@localhost:$(docker compose port postgres 5432 | sed 's/.*://')/adcp_registry" \
  WORKOS_API_KEY=placeholder-not-a-key WORKOS_CLIENT_ID=placeholder-not-a-client-id \
  WORKOS_COOKIE_PASSWORD='local-dev-cookie-password-that-is-at-least-32-chars-long' \
  PUBLIC_TEST_AGENT_TOKEN=local-token \
  TRAINING_AGENT_INVENTORY_ADSERVER_SEASON_FILE=server/tests/fixtures/inventory-sim/small-season.yaml \
  npx tsx server/src/index.ts
```

The WorkOS values are placeholders that only satisfy the server's start-up checks. `FLY_PROCESS_GROUP=web` starts the server without its background crawlers and scheduled jobs, which would otherwise reach external hosts. The tenants are at `http://localhost:3917/api/training-agent/inventory-adserver/mcp` and `.../inventory-content/mcp`, and `local-token` is their bearer token. The log shows `inventory-adserver: season small-season from server/tests/fixtures/inventory-sim/small-season.yaml`.

To start from a shipped season instead of the starter, print one as YAML. The content season, for example, has collections the starter lacks:

```bash
npx tsx --input-type=module -e "import { seasonToYaml } from './server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/season-file.js'; import { CONTENT_SEASON } from './server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/index.js'; process.stdout.write(seasonToYaml(CONTENT_SEASON))" > my-season.yaml
```

To use a season without a server, read it with `readSeasonFile` from `server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/season-file.ts`, and pass the result to `InventorySimulation.create(seed, nowMs)`, where `nowMs` is the start instant, from `server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/`.

The engine behind these tenants lives in `server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/` and has no dependencies on the rest of the test agent, so it can move into `@adcp/sdk` if that proves useful.
