<Note>
**Non-normative.** These are sandbox tenants of the public test agent, built for testing buyer and storefront agents over the life of a buy. The `sandbox_clock` controller scenario is proposed in [#9025](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/9025) and is served only by these two tenants for now. Their behaviour is a simulation, not a model of any real ad server.
</Note>

Most test agents answer each call correctly but hold fixed data. Nothing forecasts from traffic, serves a booking day by day, or delivers differently from what was forecast. The two inventory agents on the test agent do all three, driven by a seeded simulation and a clock you can move. You can test what your agent does over a 30-day flight in a few calls: when a guarantee falls behind, when actual traffic differs from the forecast, or when an episode is cancelled.

Both play the *inventory* role from [#8993](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/issues/8993): they hold the catalogue and avails, execute media buys and report delivery. A storefront agent can book through both, as their buyer, over AdCP.

## Connect

| Tenant | URL | Sells |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Ad server | `https://test-agent.adcontextprotocol.org/inventory-adserver/mcp` | Display, pre-roll video and a homepage takeover on two sites, guaranteed and in a first-price auction |
| Content platform | `https://test-agent.adcontextprotocol.org/inventory-content/mcp` | Exclusive sponsorships of a weekly podcast, a weekly newsletter and a fortnightly creator series |

Authenticate with the same bearer tokens as the rest of the test agent; see the [quickstart](/docs/quickstart). Each tenant serves the media buy and creative tasks as they are, with no new tasks:

- [`get_products`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products) returns each product with a `forecast` for the flight in `filters.start_date` and `filters.end_date` (30 days from now when omitted). `filters.countries` narrows the forecast. `filters.delivery_type` and `filters.channels` filter the products. Both `brief` and `wholesale` buying modes are served, so a storefront can read either tenant as a wholesale catalogue.
- [`create_media_buy`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/create_media_buy) books each package:
  - Guaranteed packages take `impressions` as their goal. Without it, the goal is what the budget buys at the fixed price. A goal larger than the impressions available for the package's flight and targeting, net of your other bookings, fails with [`PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-product-unavailable). The error names the goal and what is available. It is never capped. That figure is the `mid` impressions of a `get_products` forecast for the same flight and countries, requested with your account and without `budget_range`.
  - The takeover and the sponsorships take every impression of what they cover, whatever the budget. So one live booking sells the takeover for each day of its flight, and a sponsorship for each installment in it. Another booking of those days or installments fails with [`PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-product-unavailable) until that one is cancelled, which frees them. Your token's buys under every account share one market, so they compete with each other.
  - Auction packages bid `bid_price`, first-price, above the floor.
  - `targeting_overlay.geo_countries` (`US`, `CA`) narrows a package.
  - A package's `creatives` (uploaded inline) and `creative_assignments` (from your library) are assigned to it.
- [`update_media_buy`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/update_media_buy) pauses, resumes and cancels a buy or a package, and changes a package's `budget`, `impressions` or `bid_price`, or the buy's `end_time`. Raising `impressions` is checked the same way, against what the rest of the package's flight has available once the whole request is applied. Lowering it always succeeds. Cancelling the buy ignores the request's other fields, and cancelling a package ignores that package's other fields. A package's `creatives` or `creative_assignments` replace its assignments.
- [`get_media_buys`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buys) and [`get_media_buy_delivery`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buy_delivery) report what was booked and what was delivered. They include `by_package`, `daily_breakdown` and `pacing_index` for guaranteed packages. In `get_media_buys`, each package lists its `creative_approvals`.
- [`sync_creatives`](/docs/creative/task-reference/sync_creatives), [`list_creatives`](/docs/creative/task-reference/list_creatives) and [`list_creative_formats`](/docs/creative/task-reference/list_creative_formats) manage the creatives you traffic your buys with. See [Creatives](#creatives).

Everything you book is private to your credential. All the accounts you use with one credential book into the same simulated market on each tenant, so your own buys compete for inventory. A `get_products` call that names an `account` returns `cache_scope: "account"`, with forecasts net of your bookings. Provision that account with [`sync_accounts`](/docs/accounts/tasks/sync_accounts) first: an account reference the tenant never provisioned fails with [`ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-account-not-found). One without an `account` returns the public card, `cache_scope: "public"`, with forecasts against an empty market.

Neither tenant requires operator authentication, so a storefront can call them with its own credential. The publishers use `.example` domains, so a registry or DNS check of their properties fails. That is expected in this sandbox.

## Creatives

Both tenants keep a creative library for each account. Sync creatives to it with [`sync_creatives`](/docs/creative/task-reference/sync_creatives), or upload them inline on a package, and assign them to the packages you have bought.

- **Formats.** [`list_creative_formats`](/docs/creative/task-reference/list_creative_formats) returns only the formats the tenant serves: display, pre-roll video and the takeover image on the ad server; the podcast spot, newsletter sponsorship and creator brief on the content platform. Name a creative's format canonically, with `format_kind` and the `params` of one of the product's `format_options` (or its `format_option_ref`); the size of the creative's image or video picks the format when `params` is omitted. A named `format_id` copied from `format_options` also works. A request whose format still matches more than one option is refused, naming them. A creative can only be assigned to a package whose product serves its format. Otherwise the request fails with [`FORMAT_NOT_SUPPORTED`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-format-not-supported).
- **Assignments.** `assignment_operations` supports `assign`, `unassign` and `replace`. A request's operations apply together or not at all. The deprecated `assignments` field still works, and reports each failed assignment in the creative's `assignment_errors`. Merges, assignment-level trackers and landing pages, installment scoping and placement routing are rejected with [`UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE`](/docs/building/verification/compliance-catalog#error-code-unsupported-feature).
- **Review and delivery.** Every creative is approved as soon as it arrives. A buy reports `pending_creatives` while none of its live packages has a creative, then `pending_start` or `active` as usual. Delivery does not wait for a creative, though: an unassigned package delivers as the simulation's actual traffic dictates. The creatives exist to test trafficking, not to gate serving.
- **Scope.** Creatives are private to the account that synced them, like the buys they are assigned to. Each account holds up to 200 creatives of up to 32 KiB each; reference hosted media by URL. `reset_state` clears them with the rest of your market.

## Move the clock

Every time-dependent behaviour of these tenants reads your *sandbox clock*. Until you move it, it reads the wall clock. Move it with [`comply_test_controller`](/docs/building/by-layer/L3/comply-test-controller):

```json
{ "scenario": "sandbox_clock", "params": { "operation": "get" } }
```

```json
{ "scenario": "sandbox_clock", "params": { "operation": "advance_time", "to": "2026-12-31T12:00:00Z" } }
```

```json
{ "scenario": "sandbox_clock", "params": { "operation": "advance_time", "by": "P30D" } }
```

- `get` returns `simulated.simulated_now`, and `pinned: false` while the clock still reads the wall clock.
- `advance_time` takes either `to` (an RFC 3339 instant) or `by` (an ISO 8601 duration in weeks, days, hours, minutes or seconds). It returns the new `simulated_now` and `days_crossed`, the number of UTC days the clock moved across. The tenant you call runs those days at once; the other runs them on your next call to it, with the same results.
- The clock only moves forward. An earlier instant is refused with `INVALID_TRANSITION`.
- `reset_state` on either inventory tenant unpins the clock and discards your buys on both. A `reset_state` elsewhere that unpins the clock, such as `/sales/mcp`'s, also leaves your inventory buys behind: a market that has run days the clock has not reached starts fresh.
- You have one clock per credential. It is the same clock that `reporting_subscription_probe` `advance_time` moves on `/sales/mcp`, so moving it on either tenant moves it for both.

Both tenants list `sandbox_clock`, `reset_state` and the seeds below in `list_scenarios` and in `compliance_testing.scenarios`.

### Test controller seeds

A storyboard's fixtures are seeded through the same controller, so the media-buy storyboards run against these tenants:

- `seed_account` provisions an account, exactly as on `/sales/mcp`.
- `seed_product` adds a product to your market on the tenant you call. It is sold on the season's own placements, so it forecasts, books and delivers like any other product. A video fixture uses the video placements, an audio fixture the audio ones, and anything else display. A season without that channel uses its nearest installment placement instead: the creator series for video, the podcast for audio, the newsletter otherwise. The product shows the fixture's own name, channels, formats and publisher properties.
- `seed_pricing_option` prices a seeded product. A `floor_price` is the lowest bid an auction booking places, and a `fixed_price` is a guaranteed booking's rate. A seeded product with no seeded pricing option is priced like the season's own products.

Seeded products are private to your credential, like your buys. A credential that never seeds sees only the season's products. `reset_state` discards your seeds with the rest of your market.

## What happens each simulated day

When the clock passes the end of a day, that day runs:

1. **Bookings start.** A buy confirmed by the end of day N serves from day N+1, or from its flight start if that is later.
2. **Actual traffic arrives.** Each day's traffic is the expected pattern, times one deviation (the publisher's same-day deviation, its revisions and the cell deviation, added and clipped together), times a seeded daily variation, times the season's scripted events and any traffic spikes. An installment's audience also carries the episode's audience shock. These deviations are part of the traffic itself, and each forecast knows only some of them. See [How forecasts are wrong](#how-forecasts-are-wrong).
3. **Guarantees are served first.** The takeover and the sponsorships take their share. Impression goals are then served by the high-water mark method. Each package's daily target is what is left of its goal, in proportion to that day's share of its expected traffic for the remaining days. Expected traffic is the pattern every forecast starts from, so the pace does not follow any forecast's errors. The package with the least usable supply goes first, and each takes the same share of every impression it can use, never more than its target. A package that falls short catches up over its remaining days in proportion to their expected traffic, and the pace in delivery reports follows the same line.
4. **The auction takes what is left.** The highest bid above the floor wins until each package's daily budget is spent.
5. **Installments run.** Each one runs, is postponed or is cancelled, as the season says. One that runs builds its audience over its channel's curve: newsletter opens over 3 days, podcast listens over 30, video views over 6 weeks.

Delivery is reported once the day has closed and the product's reporting delay has passed: the next day for the websites, a day later for the podcast and the creator series. A sponsorship package keeps delivering after its flight ends, while its installments' audience builds.

## How forecasts are wrong

The forecast errors live in the actual traffic. The simulation draws each day's traffic with its deviations from the expected pattern already in it, and a forecast is the expected pattern plus whatever part of those deviations was known on the day it was made. A forecast made on a given day:

- **carries** the publisher's bias, as an offset from the expected pattern, and **knows** the expected pattern, every revision revealed before that day and every past same-day deviation;
- **never knows** that day's own deviation, revisions revealed later, the cell deviation, the daily variation, scripted events or a spike before it starts.

Lead time is how many days separate your sandbox clock from the day forecast. The forecast is built as seen from the sandbox day of your call.

```text
actual   = expected * exp(deviation) * daily variation * scripted events * spike tails
forecast = expected * exp(known part + b) * known spike tails

deviation  = clip(a * (same-day deviation + revisions / sqrt(lead_time_tau_days)) + cell deviation)
known part = clip(a * (past same-day deviations carried forward
                       + revisions revealed before the day / sqrt(lead_time_tau_days)))
a = channel_sigma * error_scale
b = -(bias + bias_sigma * seeded draw)
```

The error, before events, spikes and the daily variation, is `b' + (deviation - known part)`, where `b' = -b` is the publisher's bias. It widens with lead time and levels off. The revision part stops growing at `lead_time_horizon_days`, and the unknown same-day part rises with lead time, from 1 - `ar1_phi`² of a full deviation's variance on the day itself toward all of it, at a rate set by `ar1_phi`.

Everything delivery history could reveal about the publisher's deviation is already in the forecast's known part, and the part a forecast does not see is independent across days. Delivery history therefore cannot beat the forecast on that part, `deviation - known part`. The bias is a systematic offset that history can learn.

Every dial is a season key, under `forecast_error` unless noted:

- **Expected pattern.** A placement's base traffic, shaped by weekday, `trend_per_day` and the `month_shape` multipliers (twelve values, January first) under `traffic`. A season can leave out `month_shape`, which then does not change traffic. Expected traffic is the median of the actual traffic, so the mean sits slightly above it (about 1% at the shipped sizes).
- **Publisher bias.** `publishers[].bias` is how far actual traffic sits from the forecast. Negative means actual comes in below the forecast; the shipped ad server uses -0.02. `bias_sigma` adds a seeded per-publisher offset with that standard deviation, and `error_scale` multiplies the publisher's deviation. A property joins a publisher through its `publisher_id`, or is its own publisher. Publishers that are not listed have no bias and an `error_scale` of 1. The forecast carries the bias.
- **Same-day deviation.** Each publisher has a seeded deviation for each day, shared across the publisher's channels, whose neighbouring days are correlated by `ar1_phi`. `channel_sigma` sets its size per channel. A forecast knows the deviations of days that have closed, and carries them forward into the days after at the rate `ar1_phi` allows. It never knows the deviation of the day itself.
- **Revisions.** Each day, information about each later day (up to `lead_time_horizon_days` ahead) is revealed. Each revision is a seeded deviation of size `channel_sigma` (times `error_scale`) divided by the square root of `lead_time_tau_days`: the larger `lead_time_tau_days` is, the less each revision weighs and the more slowly the error widens with lead time. A forecast knows every revision revealed before its day.
- **Cell deviation.** Each cell-day carries a deviation whose size is `cell_kappa` divided by the square root of the cell's expected impressions, never counting a cell as smaller than `cell_min_impressions`. Thin cells deviate more than busy ones. It is independent across cells and days, and no forecast knows it.
- **Daily variation.** `traffic.truth_daily_sigma` is the spread of a seeded day-to-day variation of each placement. It is part of the actual traffic and no forecast knows it.
- **Audience shock.** Each installment's audience is scaled in actual traffic by a seeded shock of size `audience_sigma` (a collection key), applied across the whole audience curve of the episode. No forecast sees it. It is why content actuals differ from forecasts even though the content tenant's `channel_sigma` is 0.
- **The clip.** `log_clip` limits the deviation and the forecast's known part separately, on a log scale, in both directions. The error therefore never exceeds the unclipped error, and stays within twice the clip. The bias sits outside the clip. The clip does not limit the daily variation, spikes or scripted events.
- **Spikes.** A season can script random traffic spikes per publisher under `spikes`: how many (`per_publisher_min` to `per_publisher_max`), how large (`size_min` to `size_max`) and how much of the jump is left each day (`decay`, so a higher value fades more slowly). A negative size is a slump. A forecast made before a spike's first day does not know it, even for later days. A forecast made on or after that day knows its size and estimates its decay with an error of `decay_forecast_sigma`. The shipped seasons have none.

Scripted events are in actual traffic only. A forecast never knows an outage, a cancellation or a postponement.

### What delivery history can teach

By design, some things can be learned from what has been delivered:

- An ongoing scripted event, such as an outage that is still running.
- An episode's audience shock, after the first days of its audience curve.
- A started spike's true decay.
- The publisher's bias.

None of these helps with the deviation a forecast cannot see on the day, which is why history cannot beat the forecast on it.

### The published range

The published range is the 10th to 90th percentile of the error, to first order and without the clip. It is built per publisher, since publishers' deviations are independent of each other, and capped at twice the clip. It reflects the same-day deviation and the revisions not yet revealed, the cell deviation, the daily variation, the audience spread of installments and the forecaster's uncertainty about the decay of spikes that have already started. It excludes the publisher bias, spikes that have not started and scripted events, so actual traffic can land outside it for those reasons. [`get_products`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products) builds `low` and `high` on capacity, subtracts your bookings and caps them at what the budget buys.

### Forecasts change as you move the clock

Forecasts depend on your sandbox day where a season sets a `channel_sigma` above 0, as the ad server does. There the same `get_products` call returns different numbers after you advance the clock: the midpoint of a flight's forecast moves a little, and the band narrows as the flight gets closer. Each revision is independent of the other revisions and of the forecast already published, so one forecast gives no hint of which way the next will move. The latest forecast holds everything the forecasts made before it did. Nothing is random between calls: the same call at the same simulated time returns the same forecast.

The content tenant's `channel_sigma` is 0, so a cell-day's forecast does not change with the clock. Only the days left in the flight window and your bookings change. Forecasts differ from delivery through the cell deviation, the daily variation and the audience shocks, none of which depends on lead time. A flight that ended before the clock forecasts zero.

### Error sizes are assumptions

The shipped sizes are assumptions and have not been measured: a 6% same-day spread for display and video, 5% cell noise at the median cell on the ad server, and a -2% publisher bias. Change them in the season file. They are to be recalibrated from real publisher data.

| Key | Ad server | Content platform |
|-----|-----------|------------------|
| `channel_sigma` | `display` 0.06, `olv` 0.06 | 0 for `podcast`, `newsletter` and `creator_video` |
| `cell_kappa` | 3.08 | 0.4 |
| `cell_min_impressions` | 100 | 100 |
| `traffic.truth_daily_sigma` | 0.03 | 0.03 |
| `ar1_phi` | 0.85 | 0.85 |
| `lead_time_tau_days` | 14 | 14 |
| `lead_time_horizon_days` | 60 | 60 |
| `log_clip` | 0.693 (a factor of two) | 0.693 (a factor of two) |
| `publishers[].bias` | -0.02 for both publishers | none |
| `audience_sigma` | none | 0.12 podcast, 0.05 newsletter, 0.25 creator series |
| `spikes` | none | none |

Book exactly the forecast midpoint on `sports_hero_guaranteed`, a single-publisher product, for a 30-day flight booked 30 days ahead: it delivers under 90% of the booked amount in 7.6% of flights, about 1 in 13. That is measured as actual traffic against the forecast, so it is a floor for real bookings. Products that span several publishers miss less, because their publishers' deviations are independent. The engine's tests pin this rate to 5–11%. That range would catch a doubling of the error sizes (about 15.7%) or a halving (about 0.5%), so changing the shipped sizes needs a deliberate update of the pin.

To test how robust your agent is to the sizes, the sweep levels are ×0.5, ×1 and ×2 on `channel_sigma` and `cell_kappa` together, and 0, 0.03 and 0.06 on `truth_daily_sigma`. Run them as season files, each with its own `seed_id`. These change the actual traffic's volatility, not only the forecast's, and since expected traffic is the median of the actual traffic, its mean rises with the sizes (about 1% at the shipped sizes, about 4% at ×2).

### Versions and the market reset

Results from these tenants depend on the engine and on the season. `sandbox_clock` `get` and `advance_time` report both, inside `simulated`:

- `engine_version`: the version of the engine's behaviour.
- `seed_id` and `season_fingerprint`: the season's name, and a hash of every value in it.

Record all three with every result, and compare only results that share them. A stored market restarts, discarding its bookings, seeded products and creatives, when the engine version, the season's `seed_id` or the season's fingerprint changes. Editing a local season file therefore restarts the stored market, even under the same `seed_id`. Give an edited season its own `seed_id` to name it. Hosted seasons are fixed. The engine version is `2` and the shipped seasons' ids are `adserver-2027-lead-time` and `content-2027-lead-time`, so a market stored under any other version or id on either tenant restarts once.

The guarantee that forecasts reveal none of the actual traffic holds within one timeline. Keep the test controller (the clock and `reset_state`) away from the agent under test.

## The season

The simulation is deterministic: the same calls at the same simulated times always give the same results. The season runs from 2026-11-01 to 2028-10-30 and includes these scripted events:

| Tenant | When | Event |
|--------|------|-------|
| Ad server | 2027-01-08 to 2027-01-12 | The Summit Sports hero unit serves a quarter of its traffic. A guarantee on `sports_hero_guaranteed` falls behind, then catches up. |
| Ad server | 2027-01-20 to 2027-01-22 | Pinnacle News traffic nearly doubles. |
| Content | 2027-01-18 | The Morning Brief episode `morning_brief_012` is cancelled. Its sponsor receives nothing for it. |
| Content | 2027-01-18 | The Maker Studio video `maker_studio_006` is postponed to 2027-01-23. |

A content product lists the installments scheduled in the requested flight, with each one's `status` as known at your sandbox time: `scheduled`, then `published`, `postponed` (with the new `scheduled_at`) or `cancelled` once its date passes. A sponsorship package buys every installment of its product scheduled in its flight.

## A month in five calls

On `/inventory-adserver/mcp`:

1. Pin the clock before the flight: `sandbox_clock` `advance_time` `to` `2026-12-31T12:00:00Z`.
2. [`create_media_buy`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/create_media_buy) for 2027-01-01 to 2027-01-31 with one package on `sports_hero_guaranteed` (`pricing_option_id` `sports_hero_guaranteed_cpm`, `impressions` 180000).
3. `create_media_buy` for the same flight with one package on `preroll_auction` (`preroll_auction_cpm`, `bid_price` 10). Two buys keep each one's `daily_breakdown` separate.
4. `sandbox_clock` `advance_time` `by` `P31D`.
5. [`get_media_buy_delivery`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buy_delivery) for both buys.

In the daily breakdown, the guarantee drops to under half its usual delivery during the outage and delivers above its earlier pace afterwards. The auction package's delivery differs from the forecast [`get_products`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_products) gave for the same flight.

## Run on your own season

The two tenants run on any season you supply as a file, so you can test against your own sites, products and scripted events. Run the training agent on your machine and point a tenant at the file. The environment variables apply to the whole server, so every caller of that server's inventory tenants gets the file's season. That makes season files a fit for local and self-hosted runs. The hosted test agent serves its shipped seasons, so a season file never leaves your machine.

A season is a YAML or JSON file. Its fields are the `SeasonSeed` type in [`engine/types.ts`](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/types.ts). The starter, `server/tests/fixtures/inventory-sim/small-season.yaml`, is a complete season for one site with a guaranteed and an auction product. Copy it and edit it. The sketch below shows the shape only. It does not load on its own, because it leaves out required fields: `audience_profiles`, `segments`, `traffic` and `forecast_error` (with its `channel_sigma` for every channel a placement uses, `ar1_phi`, `lead_time_tau_days`, `lead_time_horizon_days`, `log_clip`, `cell_kappa` and `cell_min_impressions`), each placement's `name`, `formats`, `rates` and `reporting_lag_days`, and each product's `name` and `description`. The starter file is the complete, loadable example.

```yaml
seed_id: small-season
rng_seed: small-season-v1
start_date: 2026-11-01
days: 365
currency: USD
regions:
  - { region_id: us, country: US, name: United States, share: 1 }
properties:
  - { property_id: harbor_daily, name: Harbor Daily, domain: harbor-daily.example }
placements:
  - placement_id: banner
    channel: display
    property_ids: [harbor_daily]
    base_daily_impressions: 100000
    floor_cpm: 2
products:
  - product_id: banner_guaranteed
    delivery_type: guaranteed
    placement_ids: [banner]
    rate_cpm: 8
```

The loader checks:

- The shape and value ranges. Reporting lags are whole numbers, 0 or more. Collection cadences and counts are whole numbers, 1 or more. `share_of_voice` is above 0 and at most 1, and rates are between 0 and 1. Property `traffic_multiplier`, audience-profile rate multipliers, `weekday_shape` values and a collection's `audience_sigma` are not negative. The currency is an upper-case three-letter code, property domains are lower-case hostnames, floors are not negative, every placement lists at least one format, and the season has at least one placement and one product.
- A file over 5 MB, or a path that is not a regular file, is refused before it is read.
- Dates are real calendar dates, every installment of a collection falls inside the season, and so does the date an installment is postponed to.
- References that the engine leaves unchecked: product properties and collections, and the ids traffic events name.
- The forecast-error settings, checked together with the references, and listed in full with their paths:
  - Ranges. `month_shape` has twelve values above 0 and at most 100. `channel_sigma` values, `truth_daily_sigma`, `bias_sigma` and `decay_forecast_sigma` are from 0 to 5. `error_scale` is from 0 to 100, and `cell_kappa` from 0 to 1000. A publisher's `bias` is from -5 to 5. `ar1_phi` and a spike's `decay` are at least 0 and below 1. `lead_time_tau_days` is at least 0.01, and `log_clip` is above 0. `cell_min_impressions` is at least 1. A spike's `size_min` is above -1 and `size_max` is at least `size_min`. `per_publisher_min` and `per_publisher_max` are whole numbers, 0 or more, with `per_publisher_max` at least the minimum, at most the season's `days` and at most 100.
  - References. Every channel a placement uses has a `channel_sigma`, and each `publishers[].publisher_id` names a publisher that some property resolves to, once.
  - Size. `lead_time_horizon_days` is a whole number from 0 to 366, and the build stays within a budget: publishers × (`days` + horizon) × (horizon + 1) is at most 20,000,000 draws. A season at that budget can take a few seconds and on the order of 160 MB to build.
  - Finite traffic. If the build produces expected or actual traffic that is not a finite number, for example from a very large spike `size_max`, the season is refused, naming the first day and cell.
- Placement `formats` are format labels the training agent knows, such as `display_300x250` or `video_preroll`; the error for any other lists them.
- The engine's own rules. The season's `days` is a whole number from 1 to 1100, and `weekday_shape` has seven values. Region and audience-profile shares are positive and sum to 1. Event multipliers are not negative. The engine checks the forecast-error settings too. Ids are unique, and the other references resolve: product placements and segments, segment profiles, placement properties and collection placements. Each placement on the `display` or `olv` channel has `base_daily_impressions` (podcast, newsletter and creator-video placements take their audience from their collections instead), and each guaranteed product has `rate_cpm`.
- Every product covers some inventory.

The shape, reference (including the forecast-error settings) and coverage checks each list every problem they find. The engine's own rules report the first problem only.

Set one environment variable per tenant:

| Tenant | Variable |
|--------|----------|
| Ad server | `TRAINING_AGENT_INVENTORY_ADSERVER_SEASON_FILE` |
| Content platform | `TRAINING_AGENT_INVENTORY_CONTENT_SEASON_FILE` |

A tenant whose variable is unset or empty serves its shipped season. A tenant whose variable is set serves the file, and logs one line naming it. If the file is missing or invalid, the server refuses to start. The error names the variable and the path.

Editing a season file restarts the stored market on that tenant, because the season's fingerprint changes. Give an edited season its own `seed_id` to name it.

The server needs PostgreSQL. Start the one in `docker-compose.yml`, create the schema the migrations expect, then start the server from the repository root. The one-line `create schema` is enough for a throwaway local database. The [local bootstrap](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/runbooks/training-agent-gcs-reporting.md#local-bootstrap) in the runbook has the full setup.

```bash
docker compose up -d --wait postgres
docker compose exec -T postgres psql -U adcp -d adcp_registry \
  -c "create schema if not exists training_reporting_gcs authorization adcp"

PORT=3917 RUN_MIGRATIONS=true FLY_PROCESS_GROUP=web \
  DATABASE_URL="postgresql://adcp:localdev@localhost:$(docker compose port postgres 5432 | sed 's/.*://')/adcp_registry" \
  WORKOS_API_KEY=placeholder-not-a-key WORKOS_CLIENT_ID=placeholder-not-a-client-id \
  WORKOS_COOKIE_PASSWORD='local-dev-cookie-password-that-is-at-least-32-chars-long' \
  PUBLIC_TEST_AGENT_TOKEN=local-token \
  TRAINING_AGENT_INVENTORY_ADSERVER_SEASON_FILE=server/tests/fixtures/inventory-sim/small-season.yaml \
  npx tsx server/src/index.ts
```

The WorkOS values are placeholders that only satisfy the server's start-up checks. `FLY_PROCESS_GROUP=web` starts the server without its background crawlers and scheduled jobs, which would otherwise reach external hosts. The tenants are at `http://localhost:3917/api/training-agent/inventory-adserver/mcp` and `.../inventory-content/mcp`, and `local-token` is their bearer token. The log shows `inventory-adserver: season small-season from server/tests/fixtures/inventory-sim/small-season.yaml`.

To start from a shipped season instead of the starter, print one as YAML. The content season, for example, has collections the starter lacks:

```bash
npx tsx --input-type=module -e "import { seasonToYaml } from './server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/season-file.js'; import { CONTENT_SEASON } from './server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/index.js'; process.stdout.write(seasonToYaml(CONTENT_SEASON))" > my-season.yaml
```

To use the library directly, `traffic.day(d)` returns `expected` and `actual` tables. `ForecastResult.sigma` is the standard deviation of the forecast error on a log scale. Its `low` and `high` are built on capacity, net of bookings, and capped at what the budget buys. A forecast's clicks and conversions use the expected rates. To use a season without a server, read it with `readSeasonFile` from `server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/season-file.ts`, and pass the result to `InventorySimulation.create(seed, nowMs)`, where `nowMs` is the start instant, from `server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/`.

The engine behind these tenants lives in `server/src/training-agent/inventory-sim/engine/` and has no dependencies on the rest of the test agent, so it can move into `@adcp/sdk` if that proves useful.
