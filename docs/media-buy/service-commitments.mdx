<Note>
These additive contracts are new in AdCP 3.3. They are development schemas;
3.2 peers retain elapsed-time SLA semantics.
</Note>

A product can declare how quickly the seller reviews creatives and acknowledges
critical reports. Buyers compare these commitments before booking; the buy's
`available_actions[]` resolves the applicable commitment and package scope.
An absent SLA means **no commitment**, never an immediate response.
Commercial penalties and remedies remain in contracts.

Each resolved commitment covers the packages in `applicable_package_ids`
(all relevant packages when omitted). For mixed products, sellers MUST declare
a conservative bound valid across that set; one package without a commitment
prevents claiming a bound for the set. Never select the fastest package or
combine business durations from different calendars without computing a common bound.

## Creative review

`creative_policy.review_sla` uses the existing SLA window with a required
`completion_max`. Completion means an **approved or rejected decision** for a
creative in an accepted format. Ingestion, `processing`, `pending_review`, and
`suspended` do not complete review. Every applicable approval scope must reach
an approved or rejected decision; a partially approved assignment with pending
scopes is incomplete.

For example, this `creative_policy.review_sla` promises a decision within one
working day on the calendar below:

{/* Using latest because this SLA schema is unreleased. Update after release. */}
```json
{
  "$schema": "https://adcontextprotocol.org/schemas/latest/core/creative-review-sla.json",
  "clock": "business",
  "business_calendar_id": "review_team_2027_q1",
  "completion_max": "P1D"
}
```

The product value covers all accepted formats. The seller MUST carry it onto the
applicable creative entry in the buy's
`available_actions[]`: `replace_creative` or `update_creative_assignments`
routed to `sync_creatives`, or the legacy coarse `sync_creatives` action.
The action's separate `sla` continues to cover applying the mutation or ingestion.

The clock starts at seller receipt of a review submission in the product's
context, including an assignment through `sync_creatives`. A library-only upload
does not identify a product or promise package approval. Before booking, buyers
use product timing for planning; library and assignment approval remain separate.

Each review has its own clock. Changed execution or destinations requiring
review start a new clock; replay, polling, unrelated metadata, and internal
requeues MUST NOT restart one. Sellers preserve the received time, SLA, and
calendar for pending reviews. Buyers read authoritative decisions through
`list_creatives` or authenticated review notifications; `sync_creatives` status
alone cannot authorize spend.

## Business-time calendars

`SLAWindow.clock` defaults to `elapsed`, including weekends and non-working time.
An explicit `business` clock counts only working time and MUST reference exactly
one `get_adcp_capabilities.business_calendars[]` entry by `business_calendar_id`.
Missing or unresolved calendars invalidate the commitment; never fall back to elapsed time.

A calendar declares an IANA time zone, weekly working periods, complete
non-working dates, a coverage interval, and working minutes per business day:

{/* Using latest because this calendar schema is unreleased. Update after release. */}
```json
{
  "$schema": "https://adcontextprotocol.org/schemas/latest/core/business-calendar.json",
  "calendar_id": "review_team_2027_q1",
  "timezone": "UTC",
  "valid_from": "2027-01-01",
  "valid_through": "2027-03-31",
  "business_day_minutes": 540,
  "working_periods": [
    {
      "days": ["monday", "tuesday", "wednesday", "thursday", "friday"],
      "start_time": "09:00",
      "end_time": "18:00"
    }
  ],
  "non_working_dates": ["2027-01-01"]
}
```

Calendar IDs are immutable within the seller. Schedule, time-zone, closure, or
coverage changes require a new ID, declared before requests use it. Sellers
retain calendars for pending work, which keeps its original calendar and deadline.

To calculate a business deadline:

1. Verify receipt and the computed deadline fall within inclusive local-date
   coverage. Invalid time zones, overlapping or reversed periods, and closures
   outside coverage invalidate the calendar.
2. Convert `P<n>D` to `n * business_day_minutes`, then add any hours, minutes,
   and seconds as working time; years, months, and weeks are invalid. Here,
   `P1D` equals `PT9H`, while `PT24H` means 24 working hours.
3. Starting at seller receipt, consume duration only inside the union of
   half-open `[start, end)` working periods. Advance receipt outside working
   time to the next period. Non-working dates override the weekly schedule.
   Split overnight shifts at midnight;
   `24:00` is permitted only as an end time.
4. Map local boundaries through the calendar's IANA time zone. A nonexistent
   boundary advances to the first existing local instant. For an ambiguous
   boundary, use the earlier instant for a start and the later instant for
   an end. Count actual elapsed seconds inside these intervals.
5. Both maxima run from the same receipt, independently; `completion_max` is
   not additional time after `response_max`. A zero duration has the receipt
   instant as its deadline. The maxima are independent upper bounds; acknowledgement
   must still precede actual completion.

With the example schedule, a `P1D` review received on Friday,
`2027-01-08T19:00:00Z`, is due Monday at `2027-01-11T18:00:00Z`.
A `PT4H` review received Friday at 17:00 is due Monday at 12:00.
An elapsed `PT24H` received Friday at 19:00 is due Saturday at 19:00.

Duration filters MUST require presence and account for `clock`: compare UTC
deadlines for the same receipt time across business and elapsed commitments.
Without calendar support, treat business SLAs as unknown for duration matching.

For a peer pinned below 3.3, sellers MUST omit the entire business-clock SLA or
translate it to a conservative elapsed bound that holds for every request to
which that declaration applies. Dropping only `clock` and
`business_calendar_id` silently changes the promise and is forbidden. Business
calendars and the new report tasks are not declared as supported to those peers.

## Critical reports

Sellers advertise `media_buy.issue_reporting: true` when they implement both
[`report_media_buy_issue`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/report_media_buy_issue)
and [`get_media_buy_issue`](/docs/media-buy/task-reference/get_media_buy_issue).
Products advertise `report_media_buy_issue` in `allowed_actions[]`; buys resolve
it in `available_actions[]`, with `mode: "seller_managed"`. Reporting is operational
and independent of accepted commercial change rights. It does not require a
`change_terms[]` entry and carries no `change_term_id`, `constraints`, or `terms_ref`.

Issue-reporting support requires `adcp.idempotency.supported: true` with a
declared `replay_ttl_seconds`, so a lost receipt can be recovered by replaying
the original request during the replay window.

The mixed-package rule above applies to report SLAs too. A report without
`package_id` concerns the entire buy and requires an action covering all packages.
A package-scoped report cannot inherit another package's promise.

For a **critical** report, `sla.response_max` is the maximum elapsed time from
seller receipt until a **person acknowledges responsibility**. An automated
receipt does not stop this clock. For example, `response_max: "PT15M"` promises
human acknowledgement within fifteen minutes, including nights and holidays.
The [escalation SLA schema](https://adcontextprotocol.org/schemas/latest/core/escalation-sla.json)
uses the existing SLA window with elapsed time only;
`completion_max` is not part of the escalation commitment. Support without an
SLA makes no timing promise. Warnings use the same lifecycle without the critical
response promise. The seller preserves the applicable promise internally at
receipt; later policy changes and retries cannot restart or extend the clock.

A report supplements buyer controls. A buyer SHOULD pause the buy or remove
the creative where authorized and raise a report for the seller-side problem.
Reporting and acknowledgement do not pause delivery, change MediaBuy revision
or commercial terms, grant access, or settle a financial dispute.
