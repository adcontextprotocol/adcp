Who gets to spend in your name?

AI agents are starting to buy media, make creative and do business in your company’s name. trust.json is a short file on your own website that names the agents you’ve hired and the agents you operate, so sellers can tell the real ones from impostors.

Try your own domain. If there’s no list yet, we’ll show you how to start one.

The problem

Anyone can say they work for you.

When an agent asks a publisher for inventory, it names the brand it’s buying for. That’s one line in a request. Without a public source of truth, sellers fall back on paperwork, email threads and good faith. That’s slow for your real partners and an open door for everyone else.

  • Spend you never approved

    A reseller books a deal in your name, and you find out when the invoice arrives.

  • Work you’d never sign off on

    Ads generated for your brand by a team nobody briefed.

  • Partners stuck at the door

    Your actual agency waits on letters of authorization while the flight date gets closer.

Who it’s for

Built for the people who own the brand.

CMO

One answer to “who speaks for us?”

The agents you operate and the partners you’ve hired, published from your own domain and owned by your team. Change agencies, and sellers that check see it on their next refresh.

Global head of media

Your agencies, verified without the paperwork.

Give each agency exactly the reach it needs: which brands, which markets, until which date. Sellers confirm the relationship from your file instead of waiting on a letter.

Global head of creative

Your brand, in the right hands.

Creative generation is its own permission, separate from buying. The studio you hired can make work for the brand. A stranger’s claim won’t check out.

How it works

Three steps. One file.

  1. 1

    Make the list

    Name the agents you operate yourself, then the agencies and platforms you’ve hired. For each partner, choose what it can do (buy media, generate creative, measure), for which brands, in which countries, and for how long.

  2. 2

    Post it on your domain

    The file lives at yourbrand.com/.well-known/trust.json, next to your brand.json. Your domain is the proof: only whoever controls your website can put it there.

  3. 3

    Sellers check before they spend

    When an agent shows up buying for you, the seller confirms who it really is, reads your list, and checks that it’s one of yours, or hired for this brand, market and date.

Brand portfolios

One list covers every brand you own.

Publish once at your company’s domain. Each brand’s site names the parent company, and the parent lists the brand back. When both sides match, sellers use the parent’s list for every brand in the family.

A sub-brand without its own file isn’t a loophole. If nova-snacks.example never publishes a trust.json, sellers still check Nova Brands’ list. Skipping a brand in your rollout doesn’t leave it exposed.

Beyond advertising

Advertising is first. It won’t be the last.

Agents will soon book, buy, negotiate and answer customers in your company’s name. Every time, the other side needs to know the same thing: is this agent really yours, or really working for you? trust.json was built to answer that for any protocol, not just ads.

Commerce

Orders on your account

A procurement agent places orders with a supplier. The supplier checks that it runs for your company before it ships.

Customer care

Your voice, on other platforms

A support agent answers your customers inside a marketplace or messaging app. The platform checks it’s yours, or hired by you.

Travel and expenses

Bookings in the company name

A booking agent reserves flights at your corporate rate. The airline checks your list instead of trusting the claim.

Data and partners

Access to your APIs

A partner’s agent asks for your data. You check its company against the grants you published.

How it stretches: the file’s core (agents you operate, partners you’ve hired, the limits on each) is protocol-neutral. Each protocol adds its own permissions under its own name, and verifiers ignore the ones they don’t understand. Advertising’s profile is the only one defined today; the examples above show where other protocols can take it.

Stay in control

Pull access

Delete a partner’s entry, or retire one of your own agents. Sellers that check pick up the change on their next refresh. No calls, no letters.

Lock it down

Hand off the hosting

Technical guide

What your team needs to publish the file and test it with sellers. Examples use fictional companies on .example domains; replace them with your own domains and real agent origins.

The core is protocol-neutral: agents lists the agents you operate, grants lists the organizations you’ve authorized, and acknowledgements accepts grants made to you. Roles and scopes are namespaced tokens. AdCP defines the adcp: tokens and adcp profile used below; other protocols can define their own, and verifiers ignore namespaces they don’t implement.

Publish your authority in four steps

  1. Establish control of your domain

    Publish /.well-known/brand.json over HTTPS on a domain you control, or publish an authoritative_location pointer to a provider you control. Self-publishing needs no registry signup.

    If you use the AgenticAdvertising.org registry, claim your brand with its DNS TXT check. The claim controls your hosted registry entry; your domain’s own publication is what other parties use to find your authoritative record.

  2. List the organizations allowed to represent you

    Publish grants at https://{your-registrable-domain}/.well-known/trust.json. For a portfolio, publish them at the verified parent’s domain. Each grantee is an organization’s registrable domain.

    Here, Nova Brands lets one Pinnacle agent buy media for Nova Motors in the US and UK until mid-2027. This authorizes the agency. It doesn’t say Nova Brands runs the agency’s agent.

    Leave out agents to allow any of the grantee’s agents. Leave out countries and the grant is global; leave out valid_until and it has no published expiry. Set each limit on purpose.

  3. Have the agent’s operator list it

    Pinnacle lists the agent origin it runs in its own trust record, with the buying role. That origin serves its Web Bot Auth key directory at /.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory. trust.json carries no signing keys. A hosted platform lists the agents it runs in its own record.

    One agent per origin; different paths on the same origin are not separate agents. A brand that only delegates can publish grants without running an agent.

  4. Choose exclusivity, then test with each seller

    With exclusive_grants: true, your grants are the complete list: an enforcing seller rejects any uncovered third-party claim, whatever the activity. Your own agents keep their first-party authority.

    Without exclusivity, a seller may still authorize an uncovered claim from its own verified onboarding records. Once your trust record is published, legacy brand.json operator grants stop being a fallback, so move the complete list before you publish.

    Ask each seller to demonstrate an authorized request and a rejected one, checking agent, brand, account operator, scope, country and expiry. Account approval, billing and credit checks still apply.

Parent and child brands

Nova Motors names Nova Brands as its parent, and Nova Brands lists Nova Motors in its portfolio. The seller verifies both, then evaluates grants in Nova Brands’ trust record. With a verified parent, that record is the only record that starts a delegation chain; a child’s own trust record cannot override it.

How a seller verifies “we represent this brand”

  1. 1

    Authenticate the agent

    Verify its signature or an established credential mapping. A name or URL in the request is a claim, not proof.

  2. 2

    Resolve the brand

    Read its domain-controlled brand.json. Verify a claimed parent against the parent’s portfolio entry.

  3. 3

    Check the authority

    Read the parent’s or standalone brand’s trust.json. Follow at most two grants to the agent’s operator.

  4. 4

    Apply the limits

    Check activity, brand, countries, dates and any agent restriction before provisioning or spend.

If an agency uses a hosted platform, a chain can run brand → agency → platform. Every grant must cover the request, and the account operator must be the brand, its parent, or the agency. Your own agents can act without a third-party grant when both the agent’s operator and the account operator match the brand or its verified parent. The seller still verifies the agent and its operator listing. Read the grant evaluation rules →

Missing, unavailable and unauthorized are different

First work out which organization holds authority, then read its record. A missing file on a child domain says nothing about its verified parent’s rules.

The authority has never published a recordIn AdCP 3.x, a first 404 permits the legacy brand.json path, and verified seller onboarding can also establish authority. Absence never authorizes a caller on its own.
A valid record with a matching, active grantThe grant covers the claim, subject to the agent’s authenticated identity, every grant limit, and the seller’s commercial checks.

What’s authorized for my domain?

The lookup at the top of this page reads current public records, follows a verified parent, and shows published agents, grants and limits. It doesn’t authenticate a request, test a seller’s enforcement, or see a seller’s private onboarding approvals. Review these with your agency and sellers:

  • Authority: your domain or its verified parent, with matching brand.json records on both sides.
  • Agents: each origin listed by the operator that actually runs it, with the right role.
  • Grants: every organization you’ve authorized, and any restriction to named agents.
  • Coverage: brands, countries, activities, start dates and expiries for each grant.
  • Enforcement: whether your list is exclusive, and which sellers have shown you a rejected request.

Common questions

Does claiming my brand stop impersonation?

Claiming protects control of your registry entry. Stopping impersonation at the point of sale also needs a file on your domain, accurate grants, authenticated agents and a seller that checks. A registry badge or a brand name in a request is not permission to buy.

Does every brand and agent need its own file?

No. A verified parent publishes grants for all its brands in one record. The organization that runs an agent lists it in its own record. A brand that only delegates doesn’t need to run an agent or publish signing keys.

How do I revoke an agency or agent?

Remove or narrow the grant in your record. To retire an agent you run, remove its origin from your agents list. Keep the file published. Sellers recheck on their freshness schedule, so revocation lands within their cache lifetime rather than instantly. It doesn’t by itself cancel live buys or suspend an account.

Can my agency or platform host the record?

Yes. Publish an authoritative_location pointer at your own domain’s well-known trust.json location. Verifiers follow one pointer and no HTTP redirects. You stay the authority, so keep control of the domain and the hosted contents.

What are acknowledgements for?

An acknowledgement references a grant another organization published: its grantor, agent and source. The terms stay in the grantor’s record. An acknowledgement can’t authorize its author and doesn’t replace the seller’s grant checks.

Is trust.json ready for production enforcement?

It’s an experimental AdCP 3.3 surface, and SDK and seller support vary. The document name, path and schema may change. Confirm support and test the checks with each seller before relying on them. Existing 3.x integrations continue under their documented legacy rules until they adopt the trust record.
